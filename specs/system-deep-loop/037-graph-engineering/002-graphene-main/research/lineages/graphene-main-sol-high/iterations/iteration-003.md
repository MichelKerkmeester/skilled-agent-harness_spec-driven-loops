## Findings

1. **REFINE repo-1's single-authority decision: authority is a per-mode `(authority_state, authority_epoch)` selection, not a property of whichever ledger or projection is newest.** In `legacy_authoritative`, `shadowing`, and `cutover_ready`, legacy remains the sole operational authority; the typed 036 substrate and its graph projection are shadow evidence even when internally verified. Only the governed CAS into `new_authoritative_reversible` moves authority, and every later projection must expose the selected owner, state, and epoch alongside its ledger head. Therefore `GraphProjectionReducerV1` may describe typed-ledger facts before cutover but must label them `shadow` and cannot drive mode control flow, effects, convergence, or human-gate consequences. This **REFINES**, rather than contradicts, repo 1: "graph over 036 authority" is the target architecture, while the current migration contract still selects legacy authority until phase 014. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/004-architecture-coverage-and-transition-contract/003-transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy/transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:174-208] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/006-transition-authorized-ledger-core/004-transition-authorization-gateway/spec.md:52-79] **When not to use:** do not use a typed-ledger graph as runtime truth merely because replay passes, and do not infer authority from freshness, completeness, checkpoint availability, or graph topology.

2. **EXTEND repo-1's seven-plane map: the domain ledger and authorization-audit ledger are complementary evidence streams inside one authority plane, not competing authorities.** The domain stream records admitted transitions; the non-domain audit stream records both allow and deny decisions, including durable allows that never reached domain append. A trusted graph view may join them through the exact authorization reference, but domain state is reduced only from verified domain events; denials and unapplied allows appear as audit nodes/edges and cannot be promoted into domain transitions. The current reader enforces this asymmetry by refusing a domain frame whose durable audit link is missing or mismatched. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/006-transition-authorized-ledger-core/004-transition-authorization-gateway/spec.md:63-70] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/006-transition-authorized-ledger-core/004-transition-authorization-gateway/spec.md:87-98] [SOURCE: .opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/authorized-ledger/append-only-ledger.ts:553-648] **When not to use:** do not fold denial records into domain graph state, treat an allow as proof of application, or let a graph-generated relationship repair a missing authorization link.

3. **EXTEND the prior checkpoint contract with semantic-identity invalidation: a reducer version string is necessary but not sufficient.** The current registry resolves `eventType@reducerVersion`, so reusing a version label after changing reducer code could deterministically produce different bytes while still matching a checkpoint's nominal key. Checkpoint acceptance must bind an immutable reducer implementation or sealed-artifact digest, registry digest, canonicalization identity, projection schema, and every ledger-addressed configuration input; any change invalidates the checkpoint even if `graphReducerVersion` is unchanged. Historical replay must resolve the exact registered implementation or fail closed. [SOURCE: .opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/authorized-ledger/deterministic-reducer.ts:43-84] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/006-transition-authorized-ledger-core/003-replay-fingerprints/spec.md:52-74] [INFERENCE: a mutable implementation behind an unchanged version key defeats checkpoint identity because the key alone does not commit the reducer bytes] **When not to use:** do not solve code drift by accepting a same-version checkpoint, recomputing its expected digest with today's reducer, or silently relabeling old projection bytes; mint a new replay contract and rebuild.

4. **REFINE sequence-addressed point-in-time into a two-cut query: `(domainCut, auditCut, replayContractId)`.** `domainCut` answers the inclusive admitted state through domain sequence `S`; `auditCut` answers which allows, denials, and unapplied authorizations were durable at the requested audit boundary. Because the streams have separate sequences, their only safe cross-order facts are explicit links (a domain frame's earlier allow) and a caller-specified closed audit head—there is no invented global order and wall time remains metadata. The `replayContractId` distinguishes "old history under current semantics" from "the projection semantics registered at that historical cut." This **REFINES** repo 1 and iteration 002 by preventing a domain-only point-in-time snapshot from erasing denied or unapplied paths. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graphene-main/crates/graphene-core/src/fold.rs:175-201] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graphene-main/crates/graphene-store/src/lib.rs:224-229] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/006-transition-authorized-ledger-core/004-transition-authorization-gateway/spec.md:92-96] [INFERENCE: two independently sequenced ledgers cannot supply a total cross-ledger order beyond stored causal links without an explicit watermark] **When not to use:** do not answer an authorization-history question from a domain sequence alone, merge streams by timestamp, or call a current-reducer replay "what the system believed then" without naming the replay contract.

5. **REFINE the no-compaction decision: sealed packaging is not deletion authority.** Graphene's `compact()` proves only present-fold equivalence before deleting records. For 036, even byte-preserving export or segment packaging remains a copy until a governed storage migration proves canonical-byte recovery, sequence/hash-chain continuity, authorization linkage, mixed-reader parity, and retained archival readers. The existing transition policy explicitly makes archival-read proof a prerequisite to legacy retirement; it does not authorize committed domain or audit deletion. Thus the only presently safe compaction is garbage collection of disposable projections, indexes, caches, and superseded checkpoints after genesis replay succeeds. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graphene-main/crates/graphene-store/src/lib.rs:363-395] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/006-transition-authorized-ledger-core/002-typed-append-only-ledger/spec.md:100-110] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/004-architecture-coverage-and-transition-contract/003-transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy/transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:272-275] **When not to use:** do not delete committed frames after export, compression, fold-equivalence, age, or checkpoint verification; absent a separately authorized migration and retained-reader proof, keep every authoritative byte.

6. **CONFIRM repo-1's disposable-projection boundary with an operational negative test: deleting the graph must change availability/performance, never authority or accepted behavior.** After removing every graph table, index, cache, and checkpoint, verified replay must reproduce canonical graph bytes; during rebuild, consumers either use the still-selected authoritative path or return a typed `projection_unavailable/stale` result. They must never accept a stale checkpoint, caller-supplied graph object, or graph-local repair as a fallback authority. This test is stronger than structural claims that a projection is "disposable" and follows the external-anchor warning that an internally self-consistent graph can still be unverified. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graphene-main/crates/graphene-store/src/lib.rs:342-360] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/006-transition-authorized-ledger-core/002-typed-append-only-ledger/spec.md:102-110] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/blog-posts/Graph Engineering with Claude: How to Stop Running a Line and Start Running a Fleet.md:314-321] **When not to use:** do not run destructive projection deletion against production without a recoverable rehearsal boundary; in production, prove the same invariant through an isolated rebuild/shadow instance and explicit negative-control fixtures.

## Ruled Out

- **Authority by freshest representation:** a newer graph/checkpoint cannot override the per-mode authority state and epoch. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/004-architecture-coverage-and-transition-contract/003-transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy/transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:174-208]
- **One synthetic cross-ledger sequence:** domain and audit ledgers have separate sequence domains; only explicit authorization links and declared watermarks support cross-stream claims. [SOURCE: .opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/authorized-ledger/authorized-ledger-types.ts:20-76]
- **Reducer version label as complete implementation identity:** an unchanged string cannot detect code or configuration drift. [INFERENCE: exact replay identity requires immutable component digests, as the replay-fingerprint contract requires for replay-affecting machinery]
- **Archive/export implies safe deletion:** a copy does not prove long-term reader availability, audit linkage, or governed replacement of the authoritative storage surface. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/004-architecture-coverage-and-transition-contract/003-transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy/transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:272-275]

## Reflection

The most useful attack was to ask whether equal graph bytes prove equal authority history. They do not: authority owner/epoch, denials, unapplied allows, and replay semantics can differ while terminal domain state matches. The blog corpus was used only to sharpen the independent-anchor and temporal-history tests; 036 remains the authority for implementation claims.
