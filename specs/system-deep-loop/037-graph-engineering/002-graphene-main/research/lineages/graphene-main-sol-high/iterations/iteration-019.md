# Iteration 019 — When-not-to-use, rollback, shadow/canary, kill-switch, observability, and legacy-consumer boundaries for P1–P7

## Focus

Define the operational boundary for every P1–P7 contract: where it earns its complexity, where it must not be used, how it enters shadow and canary service, what may be disabled locally, what requires a governed authority rollback, which failures must be observable, and which consumers continue to speak the old contract during staged adoption. The central distinction is between **projection disablement** and **authority change**.

## Control taxonomy: three actions that must not share one switch

| Action | What changes | What must not change | Required control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Disable a derived projection or consumer** | Stop producing/reading one advisory projection; route that consumer to its named legacy signal or full replay | Per-mode authority state/epoch, writer ownership, domain/audit ledgers, effects | Local, typed feature/consumer switch; emit disabled reason, affected consumer set, last good cut, and fallback identity |
| **Abort shadow or canary promotion** | Stop dark/canary evaluation and invalidate its readiness evidence | Legacy remains sole authority; no writer or effect is promoted | Block the parity/cutover certificate, retain divergent artifacts, keep authority at `legacy_authoritative`, `shadowing`, or `cutover_ready` as governed |
| **Rollback selected authority** | Freeze admission, enter `rollback_pending`, advance epoch, fence writers, reconcile, restore legacy through a second CAS | Historical events, decisions, receipts, and failed-cutover evidence are never rewritten or deleted | The 036 per-mode rollback procedure and certificate; never a local environment flag or automatic fallback writer |

The first action is safe only when the disabled component is still advisory or has an explicitly retained legacy consumer. The second blocks promotion but does not move authority. The third is the only path that may replace a selected writer/control owner. 036 names legacy as sole writer through `shadowing` and `cutover_ready`, requires an epoch-bound CAS for every legal edge, and forbids skipped states. [SOURCE: `transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:174-208`] Its rollback procedure freezes admissions, fences writers, reconciles in-flight work, preserves history, restores legacy at a new epoch, and waits for consumers to observe it before resuming. [SOURCE: `transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:239-252`]

**INFERENCE:** implementation should expose three different discriminants—`projection_consumer_disabled`, `promotion_aborted`, and `authority_rollback_requested`—rather than a generic `GRAPH_ENABLED=false`. This prevents an operator intending to suppress bad diagnostics from silently changing writer authority, and prevents a selected-authority failure from permissively falling back without fencing.

## Rollback and canary rules across the matrix

1. **Canary units are one consumer or writer in one mode, never “the graph stack.”** P1/P2/P3 canaries are read-only or no-effect; P4/P5/P7 canaries are one reversible writer/edge and require retained legacy configuration, explicit resource fencing, an in-flight classifier, and a rollback anchor. [RELATION: REFINE repo-1 staged delivery] [SOURCE: `iterations/iteration-018.md:287-309`] [INFERENCE: consumer-sized canaries minimize independent failure domains]
2. **No automatic authority fallback.** A selected writer failure does not call the legacy writer in the same request. It freezes admissions and enters the policy state machine so the epoch and all leases invalidate together. [RELATION: CONFIRM repo-1 authority separation] [SOURCE: `transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:191-202,239-250`]
3. **Projection fallback is explicit and cheaper.** P1/P2 projection failure may route a named consumer to a retained legacy signal or full replay when that consumer's authority contract already permits it; the event must name fallback identity, reason, last verified cut, and affected decisions. It never emits an authority certificate. [RELATION: EXTEND repo-1 projection rule] [SOURCE: `orientation.md:32-40,58-69`] [INFERENCE: a typed local fallback event is required to audit consumer routing]
4. **Promotion evidence is invalidated, not edited.** Any parity divergence, failed mutant, telemetry gap, stale schema digest, or unobserved dark effect invalidates readiness and starts a fresh certificate interval; no operator waiver converts partial evidence into authority. [RELATION: EXTEND repo-1 parity] [SOURCE: `003-shadow-parity-harness/spec.md:70-75`] [SOURCE: `transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:204-208,219-231`]
5. **Rollback retains every old-contract reader through the window.** The mandatory retained set includes the legacy writer/configuration, legacy reader, dual-read adapter, upcasters, rollback switch/anchor, mixed-version fixtures, authority/cutover/rehearsal records, decision/rejection logs, reconciliation tools, and closure telemetry. [RELATION: CONFIRM repo-1 durable replay] [SOURCE: `transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:233-237`]

## Cross-contract kill-switch precedence

| Trigger | Immediate safe action | Switch class | Resume condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1/P2 projection non-convergence, corrupt checkpoint, or excessive lag while advisory | Quarantine output; use named legacy consumer/full replay | Projection disablement | Fresh verified cut/proof and shadow parity; no authority CAS |
| P3 missing/mismatched observation, mutant survival, or dark-effect leak before cutover | Abort canary and invalidate readiness | Promotion abort | New sealed case interval with zero open divergences |
| P4 stale-writer acceptance or partial protected mutation | Freeze affected admissions/effects and fence writer | Authority rollback trigger if selected | Reconciliation plus governed restoration/certificate |
| P5 dirty base or same-base conflicting commits | Quarantine base and freeze truth writers | Projection disable if preview-only; authority rollback trigger if selected writer accepted unsafe state | Clean replay/backstop and either repaired candidate policy or restored authority |
| P6 refusal reaches command/effect adapter | Reject at decoder; freeze implicated adapter | Consumer kill; authority rollback trigger only if mutation/effect escaped | Zero-mutation proof or reconciliation, decoder fix, adversarial parity |
| P7 stale decision commits or effect escapes gate boundary | Freeze gate and effect admissions; fence gate writer | Authority rollback trigger if selected | Gate-chain/in-flight reconciliation, effect recovery, restored authority epoch |
| Missing telemetry or unknown disposition during rollback window | Keep window open; freeze affected promotion/closure decision | Fail-closed evidence gate | Complete, current evidence; never infer green from silence |

## Findings

1. **REFINE repo 1 — projection disablement and authority rollback are separate state machines.** P1/P2 consumer routing can be switched locally only while the retained fallback is valid for that consumer; P4/P5/P7 selected writers require the 036 admission-freeze, epoch, fencing, reconciliation, and restoration sequence. [SOURCE: `transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:174-202,239-252`] [INFERENCE: separate discriminants prevent generic kill-switch ambiguity]
2. **EXTEND repo 1 — P1 and P2 need consumer-scoped fallbacks, not a graph-wide flag.** Belief blockers, checkpoints, diagnostics, and historical replay have different failure and recovery boundaries; disabling one must name every affected downstream decision and the retained legacy source. [SOURCE: `coverage-graph-signals.ts:580-630`] [SOURCE: `iteration-017.md:9-21,29-33`] [INFERENCE: consumer manifests are the minimum auditable routing unit]
3. **EXTEND repo 1 — P3 is both a promotion gate and a rollback sensor, never an authority actuator.** Shadow divergence blocks readiness before cutover and may trigger governed rollback after cutover, but a parity result cannot itself change authority. [SOURCE: `003-shadow-parity-harness/spec.md:70-75`] [SOURCE: `transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:219-231`]
4. **CONFIRM repo 1 — P4/P5/P7 canaries must be one reversible protected mutation surface.** A fleet-wide graph canary would combine stale-writer, truth-admission, human-context, and effect risks whose rollback and evidence differ. [SOURCE: `iteration-017.md:23-55,51-69`] [INFERENCE: one-resource/one-edge canaries preserve causal diagnosis]
5. **REFINE repo 1 — P6 can be locally disabled only while it remains an additive presentation contract.** Once P4/P5/P7 recovery depends on its stable schema, unknown or absent P6 must fail closed; it cannot degrade to success, executable advice, or an untyped retry. [SOURCE: `iteration-017.md:43-49`] [INFERENCE: schema dependency converts a presentation fallback into a safety dependency]
6. **EXTEND repo 1 — old-contract consumers are a planned rollback asset, not migration residue.** Legacy convergence, full replay, terminal/byte parity, V0 writers, historical relation replay, error clients, gate/resume adapters, and archival readers remain until their own zero-use and archival-read gates pass. [SOURCE: `transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:233-256,269-275`]
7. **CONFIRM repo 1 — failure observability blocks closure but never grants action.** Missing telemetry keeps the rollback window open; alerts and counters may quarantine, freeze, or request rollback, but only guarded consumer routing or the per-mode authority CAS changes runtime behavior. [SOURCE: `transition-versioning-and-rollback-policy.md:219-231,239-256`] [INFERENCE: observability-to-action remains an explicitly authorized boundary]

## Ruled Out / Do Not Adopt

- One fleet-wide `GRAPH_ENABLED` or `USE_GRAPHENE` switch spanning projections, parity, writers, truth admission, refusals, gates, and effects.
- Treating “fallback to legacy” as safe after new authority was selected; without admission freeze, epoch advancement, writer fencing, and reconciliation it is split brain.
- Moving authority because a shadow/canary is green, or restoring authority because a projection is red, without the 036 certificate and CAS edges.
- Auto-disabling P1 on any contradiction, P5 on ordinary serialization conflicts, or P7 on expected invalidation; these are modeled outcomes, not component failure.
- Retiring old readers when old writers stop. Historical packets, decisions, receipts, checkpoints, refusals, and approvals still require exact-version replay.
- Letting an alert, refusal, human choice, parity certificate, checkpoint, or rollback request serve as an executable transition/effect capability.
- Applying P1–P7 to direct deterministic transforms, cheap full replays, non-semantic transport/UI tests, immutable read-only writes, local parse errors, or informational acknowledgements.

## Reflection

- **What worked:** decomposing “kill switch” into projection disablement, promotion abort, and authority rollback made every failure action auditable and prevented local recovery from becoming an unauthorized writer flip.
- **What failed:** a component-by-component on/off list without consumer and authority ownership was ambiguous; the same word “disable” can mean harmlessly stop a cache, block a canary, or create split brain.
- **Ruled out:** graph-wide flags, automatic fallback writers, observability-driven authority, bundled canaries, premature legacy-reader retirement, and treating expected modeled refusals/conflicts as component outages.
