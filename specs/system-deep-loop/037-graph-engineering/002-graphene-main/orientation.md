## 1. GRAPHENE-MAIN SUMMARY

Graphene is a Rust, event-sourced work-graph coordinator with an integrated belief/truth-maintenance layer. It does not call models or execute nodes; agents perform the work while Graphene records topology, readiness, claims, outputs, beliefs, and human decisions (`GR/README.md:5-14`; `GR/specs/00-overview.md:19-38`).

Its architecture separates:

- `graphene-core`: pure model, event fold, belief cascade, IDs, budgets, and state machines—no I/O, clock, randomness, or async.
- `graphene-store`: SQLite event log, transactional fold cache, replay, and claim indexes.
- `graphene-check`: deterministic plan checks and eleven integrity gates.
- `graphene-exec`: claims, leases, checkpoints, schema-validated completion, failure, and human-node protocols.
- `graphene-server`: WebSocket notifications and read-only UI delivery.
- `graphene-cli`: the `gr` command surface (`GR/README.md:303-315`; `GR/IMPLEMENTATION.md:39-63`).

The SQLite log is authoritative; folded tables are disposable projections. The server only observes and notifies, so coordination continues through direct SQLite access if it dies (`GR/specs/00-overview.md:40-54`). Work nodes and belief nodes share one log and fold, allowing a human gate or executing node to expose not only its dependencies but whether their supporting beliefs are stale or contested (`GR/specs/00-overview.md:56-73`).

Primary capabilities include typed node contracts and bindings, deterministic readiness and skip propagation, bounded `for_each`, claim leases, read-set assertions, budgets, review nodes, consequence-bearing human gates, four-valued belief state, contradiction and retraction cascades, source-mutation staleness, point-in-time replay, structured refusals, and exact-fold golden tests. Its deliberate limits are equally important: no executor, worker pool, ontology/entity layer, scaled retrieval, tenancy, or authorization policy engine (`GR/specs/00-overview.md:122-155`).

## 2. HOW GRAPHENE APPLIES GRAPH ENGINEERING

**OBSERVED-IN-CODE — event-sourced fold.** `Record` supplies store-assigned sequence, graph, actor, informational wall time, and a typed event; the fold never reads wall time (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/event.rs:1-28`). `fold` and `fold_up_to` replay records through a deterministic, side-effect-free `apply`, reject non-monotonic sequence order, and settle derived state after every event (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/fold.rs:175-201`). SQLite's `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` surrounds read–validate–append–fold–cache as one transaction (`GR/crates/graphene-store/src/lib.rs:157-210`). This implements the blogs' trajectory and temporal-history thesis: state is derived from what happened, while old facts remain inspectable rather than being overwritten (`BLOG/What is Graph Engineering.md:144-158`; `BLOG/Eval Engineering: build the gate that lets your agents merge without you (full 6-step course).md:64-88`).

One qualification is material: `gr compact` physically deletes events after proving the current fold equivalent (`GR/crates/graphene-store/src/lib.rs:363-394`). It preserves present-state semantics, not necessarily complete audit history. That is incompatible with adopting the Graphene store literally as the 036 authority ledger.

**OBSERVED-IN-CODE — belief/truth maintenance.** Beliefs separate immutable provenance, monotonic fidelity, computed truth state, source identity, validity, sensitivity, support, contradiction, retraction, and supersession (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/belief.rs:368-445`). Truth uses `IN`, `OUT`, `BOTH`, and `NEITHER`; only `IN` is a usable premise. Distinct-source corroboration is enforced rather than trusted (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/belief.rs:136-184,420-444`).

After each event, `settle_beliefs` repeatedly classifies support and propagates withdrawal or contestation until a fixed point; a deep derived chain therefore becomes `BOTH` when its root becomes contested (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/fold.rs:693-777`; `GR/crates/graphene-core/tests/golden/cascade.jsonl:2-6`). Source mutation marks matching observations stale, invalidates active read sets, and attaches contradiction evidence to direct dependents (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/fold.rs:888-935`). This is the concrete non-monotonic mechanism missing from repo #1's higher-level `KnowledgeAssertionV1`: the blog's `contradicts`, `supersedes`, and temporal validity become executable propagation semantics rather than labels (`BLOG/What is Graph Engineering.md:144-158`).

There are two implementation gaps. `time::supersedes` correctly orders by `observed_at`, but production supersession directly closes the old validity interval without invoking it (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/time.rs:84-109`; `GR/crates/graphene-core/src/fold.rs:561-579`). Likewise, `Nogood` insertion records an impossible set, but the fold does not reject a later addition that completes it; G8 only detects an all-`IN` set afterward (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/fold.rs:614-628`; `GR/crates/graphene-check/src/gates.rs:416-438`). Thus the implemented core is rigorous about cascade, but weaker than its stated temporal and nogood admission contracts.

**OBSERVED-IN-CODE — typed work graph.** Nodes have explicit kinds, input/output schemas, bindings, dependencies, capabilities, budgets, retries, idempotency keys, and write sets. `needs` and binding sources are compared to reject fake ordering edges (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/node.rs:17-50,129-170,295-386`). This directly implements the blogs' rule that an edge exists because data moves, not because one step was narrated after another (`BLOG/Graph Engineering Roadmap.md:22-32,52-95`). Readiness is presently all-dependencies-complete; Graphene lacks repo #1's proposed `any`, `quorum`, and `stream` joins and explicit reducer algebra.

**OBSERVED-IN-CODE — leases and read sets.** Claim validation occurs inside the SQLite write lock, rejects already-owned nodes and stale premises, checks graph state and budget, and returns a claim ID (`GR/crates/graphene-exec/src/lib.rs:245-367`). This is stronger than AgentSwarms' convention-level coordination. However, `done` accepts graph, node, output, spend, and time—not the claim ID or a fencing generation—and merely checks that some active claim exists for the node (`GR/crates/graphene-exec/src/lib.rs:450-515`). **INFERRED:** after lease revocation and successor reclaim, a stale worker could submit under the successor's active-claim existence. Graphene therefore provides claim-time exclusivity, not 036-grade mutation-side fencing.

**OBSERVED-IN-CODE — refusal, budget, and human semantics.** Refusals are structured results carrying a mandatory alternative action (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/refusal.rs:1-12,18-147`). Budgets cover tokens, micro-USD, and wall time and are enforced at claim boundaries; structural limits bound nodes, concurrency, depth, rounds, fan-out, and spawning (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/budget.rs:14-83,85-180`). Human nodes declare options, context beliefs, consequence mappings, and an explicit timeout; silence never means approval (`GR/crates/graphene-core/src/node.rs:228-254`). These instantiate the self-correcting-loop requirements for defined failure paths, hard stop conditions, and human escalation (`BLOG/How to Build a Self-Correcting AI Loop That Catches Its Own Mistakes Before You See Them.md:32-61,76-99`).

**OBSERVED-IN-CODE — golden verification.** Every committed JSONL log is folded and compared byte-for-byte with an expected state; incremental rebuild and point-in-time replay are also checked (`GR/crates/graphene-core/tests/golden.rs:49-100`). This is a much better parity primitive than AgentSwarms' source-shape test and follows the eval blog's "turn failures from traces into permanent tests" rule (`BLOG/Eval Engineering: build the gate that lets your agents merge without you (full 6-step course).md:90-115`). But corpus coverage is partly nominal: the test checks required filenames, not required event patterns (`GR/crates/graphene-core/tests/golden.rs:104-127`); `claim-lease.jsonl` has no claim event, while the "out-of-order" observations are recorded with increasing `observed_at`. The methodology is strong; several fixtures do not yet prove their advertised behavior.

## 3. COMPARISON TO REPO #1 (AGENT-SWARMS)

| Loops as typed subgraphs | **WEAKER / NO CONTRADICTION** | Graphene supplies bounded retries, amendments, reviews, and fan-out, but no general typed cyclic subgraph with mode-specific convergence and terminal verdicts. Repo #1's design remains necessary (`AS1/research/research.md:61-67`). |
| Behavioral parity | **STRONGLY CONFIRMS, then REFINES** | Exact log→fold snapshots are the right foundation, but they cover one reducer surface, not cross-adapter authorized traces, and some fixtures are name-complete rather than behavior-complete (`GR/crates/graphene-core/tests/golden.rs:49-127`; `AS1/research/research.md:69-75`). |

Overall, Graphene is clearly stronger than AgentSwarms in event-derived state, belief revision, exact-fold testing, SQL-backed claim exclusivity, and actionable refusal semantics. It is weaker or intentionally narrower in execution scheduling, reducers, write isolation, effect recovery, organizational governance, typed loop subgraphs, hybrid retrieval, and cross-runtime parity.

## 4. DELTAS FOR OUR DESIGN

1. **Make the graph projection an explicit reducer over 036.** Introduce a versioned `GraphProjectionReducerV1` that accepts only gateway-verified 036 events and produces disposable graph, node, belief, claim, and human-gate views. Bind snapshots to ledger head, graph topology digest, and reducer version, following 036's existing exact-version rebuild contract (`.opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/authorized-ledger/deterministic-reducer.ts:91-135`). Do not adopt Graphene's fold-equivalent event deletion for the authority ledger.

2. **Upgrade the coverage graph from relation counting to truth maintenance.** Today it stores `SUPPORTS`, `CONTRADICTS`, and `SUPERSEDES`, while convergence largely treats contradiction as edge density (`.opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/coverage-graph/coverage-graph-db.ts:17-107,224-227`; `.opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/coverage-graph/coverage-graph-signals.ts:613-629`). Add immutable provenance, source identity, support mode, fidelity, `IN|OUT|BOTH|NEITHER`, stale status, and fixed-point `DERIVES_FROM` cascade. This complements—rather than replaces—the existing authorized contradiction/supersession projection and its `active|contested|superseded` statuses (`.opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/contradiction-supersession/types.ts:19-22,171-209`). Convergence should block on load-bearing `BOTH`, `NEITHER`, or stale premises, not merely global contradiction density.

3. **Adopt the golden-log shape, strengthen the oracle.** Use authorized event JSONL plus exact normalized projection snapshots and cross-adapter trace comparison. Each fixture must assert required event kinds and intermediate states, with safe negative controls; filenames alone are not coverage.

4. **Borrow atomic read-set validation, not lease semantics.** Graphene shows the value of checking premises and ownership inside one transaction. It does not unblock rejected waves. Retain 036's monotonic fence tokens and require the current fence on every result, retry, salvage, and terminal mutation (`specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/009-fanout-fanin-durable-orchestration/003-logical-branch-ids-leases-waves/spec.md:88-115`).

5. **Promote actionable refusals and truth-bearing human context.** Add a stable alternative/suggestion field to transition refusals. Human gates should carry current belief derivations and stale/contested status alongside 036's principals, topology/evidence digests, gate version, expiry, and fence.

## 5. RESEARCH ANGLES (PRIORITIZED)

- **P1 — Truth-maintaining convergence projection.** Determine how Graphene's four-state, fixed-point cascade can replace contradiction-density proxies with premise-aware convergence. Examine `GR/crates/graphene-core/src/belief.rs:136-184,368-445`, `GR/crates/graphene-core/src/fold.rs:693-777`, `BLOG/What is Graph Engineering.md:144-158`, the coverage-graph schema/signals, and `runtime/lib/contradiction-supersession/`. **Yield:** `BeliefProjectionV1`, its cascade algorithm, and explicit convergence rules for stale, contested, withdrawn, and unresolved evidence.

- **P2 — 036-ledger-to-graph fold contract.** Extract the minimum event, reducer-version, checkpoint, rebuild, and point-in-time semantics needed to make graph state disposable without creating a second authority. Examine `GR/crates/graphene-core/src/event.rs`, `fold.rs`, `GR/crates/graphene-store/src/lib.rs:157-210,342-394`, the temporal-memory blog, and `runtime/lib/authorized-ledger/`. **Yield:** `GraphProjectionReducerV1` plus an audit-preserving compaction/non-compaction decision.

- **P3 — Adversarial golden parity harness.** Audit every golden fixture against its claimed mechanism, including intermediate truth states and refused operations, then map the pattern to cross-adapter 036 traces. Examine `GR/crates/graphene-core/tests/golden.rs`, all golden JSONL/expected pairs, `GR/specs/10-verification.md:85-104`, `BLOG/Eval Engineering...md:90-115`, and the 036 shadow-parity contracts. **Yield:** a fixture matrix with event-shape assertions, external expected outcomes, timing permutations, and negative controls.

- **P4 — Lease-to-fence safety proof.** Reproduce stale worker → expiry/revocation → successor claim → old worker completion, and trace every mutation API that omits claim identity. Examine `GR/crates/graphene-exec/src/lib.rs:245-515`, `graphene-store`, CLI claim/done commands, concurrency tests, `BLOG/Graph Engineering Roadmap.md:195-217`, and `runtime/lib/locks-and-fencing/` plus `branch-leases-waves/`. **Yield:** a decisive mutation API rule requiring claim ID and monotonic fence, and a test determining whether any Graphene mechanism can safely inform waves.

- **P5 — Temporal supersession and nogood contract audit.** Resolve the gaps between specified and implemented out-of-order supersession and add-time nogood rejection. Examine `GR/crates/graphene-core/src/time.rs:84-109`, `fold.rs:561-579,614-628`, G8/G9, the `nogood` and `out-of-order` fixtures, `BLOG/What is Graph Engineering.md:144-158`, and the 036 contradiction/supersession projection. **Yield:** precise temporal ordering and nogood-admission rules, including refusal behavior and required fixtures.

- **P6 — Actionable refusal protocol.** Test whether "refusal as a result with a mandatory alternative" improves safe recovery across graph compilation, claims, budgets, gates, and belief revision. Examine `GR/crates/graphene-core/src/refusal.rs`, `GR/skill/references/failure.md:1-46`, the self-correcting-loop failure-path guidance, and 036 gateway/error contracts. **Yield:** `TransitionRefusalV1` with stable code, typed detail, suggested action, retryability, and authority boundary.

- **P7 — Human gates over live belief context.** Study how consequence maps, explicit timeout policy, and cold-context truth state should compose with 036's durable fenced gate entity. Examine `GR/crates/graphene-core/src/event.rs:116-141`, `node.rs:228-254`, `GR/skill/references/human-nodes.md`, the self-correcting/eval blogs, and 036 receipts/effect recovery. **Yield:** a human-gate projection that exposes why, stale/contested premises, answer-to-edge mappings, timeout events, and mutation-side fencing without permitting implicit approval.
