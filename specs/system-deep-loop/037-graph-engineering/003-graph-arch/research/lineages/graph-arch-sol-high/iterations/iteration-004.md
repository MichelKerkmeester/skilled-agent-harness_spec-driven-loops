# Iteration 4: Sealed Materialization and TOCTOU Closure

## Focus

This iteration derived the immutable compiled-graph artifact that must sit between `GraphAdmissionProofV1` and execution, then separated its content guarantees from the live authorization, budget, approval, fence, and effect checks that remain mandatory. The selected interpretation is the materialization slice of the broader remaining policy/approval/refusal/budget question; policy compilation and budget lifecycle deserve later dedicated passes.

## Findings

1. **A compiled object is not a sealed artifact — EXTEND Decision 1's typed executable IR and REFINE iteration 2's four-boundary chain.** GraphARC verifies admitted status and a proposal fingerprint, then resolves bodies, writes, construction flags, trace, budget, and state schema from the live `Materializer` and registry. The returned `CompiledGraphARC` has no durable canonical manifest or attestation. Therefore system-deep-loop needs a content-addressed `SealedCompiledGraphV1` manifest produced before execution; an in-memory compiled object is only an implementation product. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/materialize.py:204-282] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/003-graph-arch/research/lineages/graph-arch-sol-high/iterations/iteration-001.md:11]

2. **The seal must bind executable meaning, not topology alone — CONFIRM Decision 1 and EXTEND iteration 3's admission dependency set.** Proposal identity covers serialized proposal bytes, but node bodies come from registry factories, arguments may be dropped or forwarded, writes come from an operator table or body attribute, dynamic destinations are generated by runtime bodies, and compiler/materializer options influence execution. Seal inputs must therefore include the full proposal and topology digests plus frozen registry/capability snapshot, per-node body artifact and factory-contract digests, canonical typed arguments, effective read/write sets, state/reducer schemas, static and dynamic destination allowlists, adapters/gates/effect intents, compiler/materializer/kernel versions and flags, and the admission-proof digest. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/proposal.py:117-127] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/materialize.py:213-237] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/materialize.py:431-455]

3. **Registry-owned bodies need immutable artifact identity, not merely a frozen name map — REFINE Decision 7 and EXTEND iteration 2's executable trust-root finding.** GraphARC correctly prevents proposal-supplied bodies and refuses a kind removed after admission, while `freeze()` prevents later registration. But a frozen registry map does not by itself prove the bytes, environment, dependencies, or factory output of a callable. Each node instance must resolve from a trust-owned body artifact digest and reproducible build contract; if a closure or process-local factory cannot be canonically identified, materialization must occur inside the trusted execution boundary and the resulting executable must not be exported as a portable seal. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/materialize.py:11-19] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/materialize.py:433-455] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/tests/test_planner_loop.py:475-485] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/tests/test_planner_loop.py:1108-1124]

4. **Immutable content sealing and runtime-fresh authority are different predicates — CONFIRM Decision 2 and CONTRADICT any “valid seal means runnable now” interpretation.** Artifact verification proves that executable content has not changed; it does not prove current actor/capability, authority epoch, policy, approval, budget reservation, domain head, resource version, lease, fence, or effect permission. A byte-identical artifact can become ineligible without becoming corrupt. The runtime must resolve those facts independently, and a change requires fresh authorization or approval; only a content-affecting change requires recompilation and a new seal. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/003-graph-arch/research/lineages/graph-arch-sol-high/iterations/iteration-001.md:15] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/036-deep-loop-innovation/006-transition-authorized-ledger-core/004-transition-authorization-gateway/spec.md:87-98] [SOURCE: .opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/locks-and-fencing/locks-and-fencing-types.ts:64-109]

5. **Human approval must bind the sealed consequence and live dependency vector — REFINE Decisions 3 and 4 and EXTEND Graphene P7.** GraphARC's loop asks approval after materialization and passes the proposal plus admission result, but the recorded request contains only proposal identity, fingerprint, nodes, edges, round, and goal; its callback result is process-local and execution follows immediately. A durable `GraphApprovalDecisionV1` must bind artifact digest, admission-proof digest, exact consequence/gate, principal and role, policy identity/digest, authority epoch, budget reservation/head, protected resource versions, expiry, and one-shot decision identity. Execution revalidates all of them; approval is evidence for the gateway, never a bearer capability. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/loop.py:721-764] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/loop.py:792-847] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/003-graph-arch/research/lineages/graph-arch-sol-high/iterations/iteration-001.md:17]

6. **Protected writes close TOCTOU only at the mutation boundary — CONFIRM Decision 2's mutation-side fencing and REFINE the sealed graph's declared-write role.** The seal supplies canonical resource/read/write intent for authorization and lock planning, but it cannot freeze a live resource. The existing fencing layer binds a lease to the exact protected ledger, revalidates the current fence, compares the verified head with the expected head, mints an unforgeable fence capability, and only then calls the single-use authorized append. Every graph-originated domain write or effect must use the corresponding fenced primitive; a declared write set is not mutation authority. [SOURCE: .opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/locks-and-fencing/fenced-ledger-writer.ts:30-83] [SOURCE: .opencode/skills/system-deep-loop/runtime/lib/locks-and-fencing/locks-and-fencing-types.ts:64-109] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/003-graph-arch/research/lineages/graph-arch-sol-high/iterations/iteration-001.md:15]

7. **GraphARC's local guards become explicit governance mutants for the combined design — CONFIRM Decision 8's mutant corpus and EXTEND it across both seal and live-check boundaries.** Existing tests already reject swapped proposal content, subclass fingerprint lies, removed registry kinds, undeclared writes, and unadmitted destinations. The system design must additionally prove body replacement under the same registry key, post-admission argument change, writes-table widening, compiler-flag change, stale policy/approval/budget dependency, forged admission result, stale head/fence, and effect execution without an effect-specific authorization. Each mutant must fail at its earliest owning boundary and leave zero unauthorized domain, budget, projection, or effect mutation. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/tests/test_planner_loop.py:320-340] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/tests/test_planner_loop.py:1080-1105] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/tests/test_planner_loop.py:1108-1124] [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/003-graph-arch/research/lineages/graph-arch-sol-high/iterations/iteration-001.md:23]

## Seal Inputs

1. Canonical current-version `GraphIRV1`, including proposal bytes, topology, typed ports/state/reducers, gates, joins, failure edges, capabilities, effect intents, and canonical protected-resource intent.
2. Verified `GraphAdmissionProofV1` identity and digest, not a caller-supplied `AdmissionResult`.
3. Frozen organization snapshot: org graph, registry/capability catalog, policy document and compiled policy provenance.
4. Reproducible body artifacts and factory contracts for every node instance; same registry key with different body digest is a different build.
5. Validated, canonical typed arguments. Raw `forward_args=True` is ineligible for sealing.
6. Effective reads/writes after operator declarations and body metadata are resolved; undeclared body writes remain kernel violations.
7. State schema, reducer, checkpointer, adapter, compiler, materializer, kernel, and compilation-option identities.
8. Budget estimate and reservation request identity, but not the live reservation balance or final debit.
9. Canonical serialization rules, full content digest, issuer/build provenance, and attestation.

[INFERENCE: seal inputs enumerate every live producer consumed by GraphARC materialization at specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/materialize.py:213-282 and every omission named by Decision 1]

## Execution Revalidation Sequence

1. Decode the closed artifact schema, reject unknown/extra fields, recompute `content_digest`, authenticate issuer/build, and verify compiler/materializer/kernel compatibility.
2. Match the exact artifact, proposal, topology, run scope, and admission-proof digests to the execution request; independently verify the admission proof and its current dependencies.
3. Resolve trust-owned body artifacts, argument schemas, state/reducer schemas, registry snapshot, compiled policy provenance, adapters, and capability/effect declarations; compare every digest to the seal before loading executable code.
4. Load current authority state: actor/principal, capabilities, authority epoch, transition policy, domain head/resource versions, budget reservation/head, and required human decisions. Reject stale or ambiguous facts without mutating domain state.
5. Revalidate each human decision against artifact digest, exact consequence edge, policy and dependency vector, expiry, principal/role, and single-use identity.
6. Acquire leases in canonical resource order for the artifact's protected write/effect set; compare resource identities and expected versions/heads. Read-only resources need consistency checks but no mutation fence.
7. Request 036 authorization for graph start/resume and for each governed transition or effect at its owning boundary. An intact seal is evidence input, not an allow decision.
8. Instantiate only sealed bodies with sealed canonical arguments, effective writes, state/reducer contracts, and allowed destinations. Invoke through the kernel; enforce declared writes, capability controls, budget meters, and runtime-confined `goto` against the sealed destination set.
9. At each domain/effect commit, revalidate the current lease/fence, head/version, authority/policy/approval freshness, exact event/effect digest, budget availability, and single-use authorization proof under the guarded primitive.
10. Append the domain/effect receipt, record actual debit/refund, and emit projections only from committed ledger facts. Any mismatch denies or refuses with zero unauthorized mutation and requires a fresh appropriate proof, authorization, or build.

## When Not to Use

- Do not introduce this artifact for a small deterministic harness action, local parser, or bounded retry with no dynamic graph proposal, independent roles, governed edges, or protected state. Use the owning harness and ordinary transition contract. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/blog-posts/Harness, Loop, or Graph? Building Reliable Agent Systems at the Right Layer.md:52-72]
- Do not create a second certificate for a statically deployed graph already represented by an equivalent immutable, attested deployment artifact; extend or profile the same artifact contract.
- Do not require mutation fences for read-only projections or immutable content-addressed writes that cannot overwrite protected identity; still verify artifact content and current read consistency when correctness depends on it. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/003-graph-arch/research/lineages/graph-arch-sol-high/iterations/iteration-001.md:15]
- Do not claim portable sealing for process-local closures, mutable interpreter state, or factories without reproducible body identity. Materialize and execute inside the trusted boundary instead.
- Do not mutate a sealed work graph in place. Dynamic work graphs are ephemeral, but each topology patch is a new proposal, admission proof, artifact digest, and authorization request; stable organization policy remains separately versioned. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/blog-posts/Graph Engineering: After Loops, This Is How You Wire Multi-Agent Orgs.md:124-170]
- Do not treat the artifact, admission proof, or human approval as permission for effects, writes, budget debit, resume, or future graph versions.

## Ruled Out

- Treating the returned `CompiledGraphARC` object as durable proof: it has no canonical manifest, content digest, or trust-separated attestation. [SOURCE: specs/system-deep-loop/037-graph-engineering/context/graph-arch/grapharc/planner/materialize.py:255-282]
- Sealing only proposal topology: registry bodies, arguments, writes, schemas, reducers, compiler flags, policies, gates, and effects also determine executable meaning.
- Reusing approval solely because artifact bytes remain unchanged: policy, authority, assignment, budget, resource versions, and expiry may have changed.
