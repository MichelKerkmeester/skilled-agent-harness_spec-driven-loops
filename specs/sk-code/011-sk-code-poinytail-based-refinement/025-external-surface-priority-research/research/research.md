## 1. Verdict

Round one's verdict stands and round two sharpens it. The surface order needs no new field and no second order source: the order lives in one authored list, `hub-router.json:7` `routerPolicy.tieBreak`, which the compiler copies verbatim and the doctor already validates (F1, F2, F4). Read as the operator described it, the shipped list already says the right thing for the operator's own repository: the native surface sorts ahead of the two examples. What round one found missing was the rule for a surface the user adds. Round two found that the sanctioned scaffold path does not apply that rule either: it writes `tieBreak` membership with no placement rule, so a surface added through `/create:skill-parent update --surfaces` lands last and loses every keyword tie (F47, R1).

Round two also changes the shape of the problem in four ways. First, the order is not the only gap: the removal path is a multi-store procedure that no single gate verifies, it needs two authored copies nobody names (the activation manifest and the canary fixture under `specs/sk-doc/z_archive/019-skill-routing-refactor/.../015-router-unification-program/`), and it leaves hand-edit legs that no generator owns (F53, F54, F61). Second, the health signals mislead while that broken state persists: `parent-skill-check` is green exactly when served routing is stale and doc links are dead (F56), `compiled-route-guard.cjs` false-greens when the authored root is absent (F41), and editing the registry without a re-mint silently degrades routing to a legacy stub with no message (F51). Third, the stack-agnostic intents are Webflow-dominated, so deleting the examples cuts real coverage for DEBUGGING, PERFORMANCE, DEPLOYMENT, ACCESSIBILITY and IMPLEMENTATION until three orphan shared leaves are mapped (F35, F37, R6). Fourth, the first thing an external user reads tells them to replace the native surface as well and to hand-edit a generated projection (F76, F77).

The recommended work is: R1 (write the insertion rule where surfaces are added, and make the scaffold apply it), R2 (make the stack-agnostic tooling stop naming the examples, which needs a minted union-child discriminator), R3 (make removal a documented, verified path with both authored copies and corrected doctor messages), R4 (mark the two surfaces as examples), R6 (map the three orphan shared workflow leaves), R7 (fix the two specs/-dependency failure modes and document the keep-rule), R8 (name the three follow-ups the scaffold never runs), R9 (one external-user guide inside the README adaptation block), R10 (name the stale-policy failure instead of serving the silent legacy sentinel). R5 is decided by the operator: sk-code-opencode's generic language vocabulary stays as it is.

## 11. Caveats on evidence quality

- **Source of truth.** The merged findings registry carries 4 keyFindings for 40 iterations, so the iteration files are the source for every claim above; `findings-registry.json` was not used as a content source.
- **What the tags mean.** `confirmed (orch run)` and `confirmed (copy run, lineage X)` describe what the cited record reports. This synthesis ran no research command of its own and did not re-execute any copy experiment.
- **Executor findings are hypotheses.** A finding tagged `executor-reported` has no independent rerun and should be treated as a hypothesis until a phase in section 9 exercises it.
- **Lineage D executor change.** Lineage D ran cli-codex `gpt-6-luna` at effort max, tier fast for iterations 1 to 4, then pi `openai/gpt-6-luna` xhigh fast for iterations 5 to 10, so the second half of that lineage is a different runtime.
- **Lineage C's copy boundary.** Iteration 6 reports that at least some of its "copy" commands expanded `$X` to empty and ran against the real worktree. Its gate results (the removal checklist, the sync deadlock, the copy-back fix) are consistent either way, but they may reflect the live tree. Its self-reported recovery list is recorded in section 10.
- **Lineage D iteration 4 read a damaged tree.** Its copy showed a five-mode `tieBreak` while the worktree had three modes; that is the lineage C scope violation, so D iteration 4's copy result is scoped to the prepared base and its worktree reading must not be cited as the pristine state.
- **Reduced-scope retries.** Lineage D iterations 1, 2, 6 and 7 are reduced-scope retries: iteration 1 did not run a runtime load trace, iterations 2 and 6 could not dispatch the slash workflow, and iteration 7 ran no copy probe. Iteration 8 has status timeout: the add-surface leg and the full CI suite were not run.
- **Slash-command limits.** Lineages B, C and D could not execute `/create:skill-parent update --surfaces` through a command runtime; their `--surfaces` results are YAML-driven simulations or contract reads. Only the orchestrator observed the command path's leg outcomes (section 10).
- **Copy recipe exclusions.** Most round-two copy recipes exclude `specs/`, so `compiled-route-sync.cjs` could not be exercised in them; sync results come from copies that included the authored closure.
- **Quoted messages.** Command output is quoted as the iteration files recorded it; some filter or overflow losses are noted in those files and are not restated here.
- **Scope.** This research concerns the sk-code hub and the surfaces it bundles. The `mcp-obsidian` MCP tool, the `sk-design-md-generator` webflow brand bundle, historical benchmark reports and changelogs are unrelated collisions, not dependencies (round 2 C iteration 7).
