## Reflection

- What worked and why: reading the checklist's Scope section first, before any coverage grep, produced the finding that explains every other one. The scope statement is the set's own statement of what it is for, and once it was read the zeros stopped being a list of omissions and became a single decision. That is the difference between a coverage pass that produces forty edits and one that produces a scope edit plus a short list.
- What did not work and why: three earlier findings were overstated because they were derived from lowercase crate-name greps and from reading a probe as a policy. `indexmap` versus `IndexMap`, and the `rg` panic probe versus the clippy deny list, are both cases where the grep answered a narrower question than the one asked. The corrections cost a third of this iteration and were worth it.
- What I would do differently: in every coverage iteration, read the target's own scope or purpose statement before grepping for terms. A document that says what it is for tells you in one paragraph which absences are decisions and which are gaps, and it is the only way to tell them apart.
