⏵ Thought                                                                                                                    

  A) A small LRU inside the reader keeps the cache next to the data it caches, so invalidation stays local and you can reason about correctness from one file. The trade-off is coupling: every reader
  instance carries its own cache, so you can't share entries across readers or tune the policy centrally, and the reader's tests now have to account for cache state.

  B) A memoized wrapper around the reader keeps the reader pure and lets each call site opt in, disable, or swap the cache. The trade-off is staleness risk: the wrapper keys on arguments, so any
  journal mutation that bypasses it will leave callers reading entries that no longer reflect the file.

  Which one do you want?

⏵ Thought                           

  Two options, as asked:

  A) Small LRU inside the reader. The cache sits on the read path itself, so every caller gets it for free and there's no way to accidentally bypass it. The trade-off is that the reader becomes
  stateful: eviction policy is now welded to the read logic, which makes the reader harder to test in isolation and means you can't opt out for a caller that genuinely needs a fresh read.

  B) Memoized wrapper around it. The reader stays pure and the cache becomes a composable layer you can apply, swap, or skip per call site. The trade-off is that correctness now depends on every
  caller remembering to go through the wrapper, and memoization keyed on arguments needs explicit invalidation or it will serve stale journal entries after a write.
