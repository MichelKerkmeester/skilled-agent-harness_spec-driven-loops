In plain terms, those documents are my rulebook for working in this repository. They govern how I work, not what we work on.

The pile looks dense because of how it is organized. There is one rule file per kind of step: one for adding code, one for touching files outside the ask, one for calling work done. Before I take a step, I read the file that matches it. When nothing matches, none of these files apply.

Four of the rules shape what you see from me:

1. The answer comes in the first line. Evidence and caveats follow it.
2. Length tracks your question. Filler does not survive the cut, and chat replies carry no tables.
3. When you say you did not follow, I rewrite the explanation rather than repeat it. This reply is that clause, applied.
4. Every turn ends by naming what is yours to do next, or by saying nothing is.

The remaining files cover the work itself. They decide what counts as proof before I call work done, when I stop to ask rather than guess and what I may change without your say so. You see their results, not their text.

The comment directive, in the same terms: a comment explains why the code exists. It never cites the ticket, requirement or spec folder behind the change, because the ticket dies and the why does not. The pre-commit gate blocks a violation.

Nothing here needs your decision. I edited no files. The one open item is the question the session started with: what would you like to work on?
