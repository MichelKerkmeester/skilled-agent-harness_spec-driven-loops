QUESTION
What should the system inject at an EVENT, rather than at a prompt? An event is something
that just happened: work completed, a goal was met, a turn ended, a session ended, a gate
passed or failed.

WHY THIS IS OPEN, AND WHAT IS ALREADY SETTLED
A prior ten-iteration round asked whether to inject rule content keyed on the USER'S
PROMPT VOCABULARY. It refused eighteen candidates and the refusal stands. Do not
re-propose any prompt-vocabulary trigger; that question is closed.

This round asks a different question the prior round never tested. An event trigger has a
silence condition for free: the event is rare and discrete, so the hook is quiet by
default. That is exactly what every refused candidate lacked.

GROUND TRUTH — verify, then build on it, do not rediscover it
Read `.opencode/hooks/injection-contract.md` section 4 in full. Three facts matter:
- The Completion Evidence Sentinel already DETECTS when a completion claim looks
  unsupported by evidence, and injects nothing. Channel `[LOG]`. Advisory-only was an
  explicit v1 decision, not an oversight.
- Session Stop runs and injects nothing. Channel `[NONE]`.
- The goal hook already reads BACKWARD COMPOSITION at turn end, flattening the ending
  message plus tool results into evidence. That mechanism is proven and running.
So there are live detectors with their output channel switched off. Whether to switch any
on is a policy change, and one prior candidate was refused specifically for upgrading an
advisory log to an injection without consent. Treat that as a real cost, not a formality.

THREE THINGS TO COVER, IN THIS ORDER
1. THE OPERATOR'S FIRST IDEA: when work completes or a goal is met, inject guidance on how
   to present final findings. The repo has rules for this that bind only on write turns.
   Does the completion event reach them, and is the guidance worth the channel?
2. THE OPERATOR'S SECOND IDEA: smartly trigger the structured question tool. Note the
   timing problem before you evaluate it: deciding a turn SHOULD have asked requires
   judging a reply that does not exist at prompt time, and at turn end that turn is over.
   Say plainly whether that is fatal or solvable.
3. CANDIDATES THE OPERATOR DID NOT NAME. This is a required section, not optional. Survey
   every event the runtimes actually expose and ask what each one is uniquely positioned
   to carry. Propose at least three the operator has not thought of, and hold them to the
   same bar. An obvious idea nobody proposed because it is bad is also a finding.

THE BAR — apply it, do not re-derive it
`injection-contract.md` records that two prompt-time directives were RETIRED for
"restating a disposition every turn", and that the survivor stays "because it is not a
disposition — it names a specific prohibition a pre-commit gate enforces, and the gate is
what makes it load-bearing." Every candidate says which side it falls on.

## 3. IDEA TWO — structured question triggering
- **The fleet's own re-ask precedent defines the boundary.** The one machine-authored question (Gate 3) is *fixed-content* and *forward-looking* ("before any Write/Edit, pick one", `injection-contract.md:72-84`), and its boundary re-arm requires a **stable question identity** — receipts confirm only "a question hash with `lifecycleEpoch >= 1`" (`spec-gate/README.md:30`). A missed-ask detector inverts every property: content composed on the fly from the reply it judges, direction backward (about the turn that just ended), identity one-shot. The re-arm machinery at compaction (`system-spec-gate.js:183-187`) cannot carry it; it exists precisely because its question never varies.
- **Authorship is the fatal link (iteration 5's finding, generalized this pass).** Each ship-approved ask is (a) authored by the harness *before* the model composes and (b) fixed-enum — Gate 3's A–E, the mid-run deny reason. A useful missed ask is (a) authored in the model's head and (b) unbounded. Any detector can find "no question in the reply" (recall); none can find "should have been one", because the alternatives — and the counterfactual reply — never existed outside the model.
- **Standing legs:** three-condition bar (`handoff-and-questions.md:101-106`), prose-is-compliant default (`:108-110`), per-runtime surface table with unverified rows plus the no-invented-names rule (`:125-131`, `:138-140`), and the rewrite surfaces (Pi `message_end`, OpenCode `chat.message`) = authorship violation on two runtimes (NC-C; iteration 4, inherited).
