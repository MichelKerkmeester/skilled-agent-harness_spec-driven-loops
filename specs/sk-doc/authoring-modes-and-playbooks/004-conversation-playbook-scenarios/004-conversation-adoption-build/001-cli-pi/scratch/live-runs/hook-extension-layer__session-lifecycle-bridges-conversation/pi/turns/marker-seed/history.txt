 ● 60-Item Checklist — Preserving Session Decisions                                                                                                                                                     
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   Capture Fundamentals (1–10)                                                                                                                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   1. Log every decision the moment it's made. Delayed capture loses the conversational context that justified the choice. A decision not written down within the session is a decision you'll          
      re-litigate tomorrow.                                                                                                                                                                             
   2. Record the decision in one sentence, active voice. "We will use X for Y" beats a paragraph of narrative. One sentence forces clarity about what actually changed.                                 
   3. Name the decision explicitly. Give each entry an identifier and a short title. Unnamed decisions become invisible the moment the session ends.                                                    
   4. Separate decisions from discussion. Discussion is exploration, decisions are commitments. Mixing them makes the record unreadable six weeks later.                                                
   5. Capture the date and session marker. Timestamp every entry with the marker so recovery can locate it. Undated decisions can't be ordered when conflicts emerge.                                   
   6. Mark who decided. Person or role, not "the team." Ownership determines who resolves future contradictions.                                                                                        
   7. Note the decision status. Proposed, accepted, superseded, or rejected — each needs a distinct label. Status prevents stale choices from being treated as current.                                 
   8. Write for a reader who wasn't there. No session shorthand, no unexplained acronyms. The record's only test is whether an outsider can act on it.                                                  
   9. Capture small decisions too. Minor choices compound into architecture. The trivial-looking decision is usually the one nobody remembers making.                                                   
   10. Do it continuously, not at the end. End-of-session summaries lose 80% of the reasoning. Capture happens in the gaps, not in a final sweep.                                                       
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   Rationale & Alternatives (11–22)                                                                                                                                                                     
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   11. Always record the why, not just the what. Reasoning is the expensive part to reconstruct. What without why is a rule with no expiry condition.                                                   
   12. List the alternatives that were rejected. Each rejection is a future question already answered. Without it, someone will propose the same option again.                                          
   13. Say why each alternative was rejected. "Considered and dropped" is useless. The disqualifying constraint is the reusable artifact.                                                               
   14. Record the constraints that drove the choice. Time, budget, tooling, compatibility, politics. Constraints expire, and decisions should be revisited when they do.                                
   15. Note assumptions explicitly. An unstated assumption becomes a silent bug in the decision record. Flag them so they can be tested and invalidated.                                                
   16. Distinguish evidence from opinion. Mark what was measured versus what was judged. Readers need to know how much weight the rationale carries.                                                    
   17. Record what would change the decision. Give each decision a falsifier or reversal trigger. This is what makes a decision reviewable rather than dogma.                                           
   18. Capture the trade-offs accepted. Name what you knowingly gave up. Accepted cost is a decision; unacknowledged cost is a debt.                                                                    
   19. Note the confidence level. Low-confidence decisions deserve periodic review. High-confidence ones shouldn't consume review budget.                                                               
   20. Record open questions attached to the decision. Partial decisions need their gaps labeled. An open question is a decision in waiting.                                                            
   21. Preserve dissenting views. Disagreement is early-warning data. Erasing it removes the record's ability to predict failure.                                                                       
   22. Link the evidence sources. Files, tickets, docs, benchmarks, conversation references. Unlinked rationale can't be verified or extended.                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   Naming, Structure & Hygiene (23–32)                                                                                                                                                                  
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   23. Use consistent entry structure. Same fields, same order, every time. Predictable shape is what makes a record searchable rather than merely present.                                             
   24. Keep entries short and scannable. If it needs a heading tree, it's a doc, not an entry. Brevity is a retrieval feature.                                                                          
   25. Use stable identifiers. Never renumber or reuse IDs. References in code, docs, and conversations depend on stability.                                                                            
   26. Write in the project's current vocabulary. Stale terminology makes records unfindable. Update vocabulary deliberately, not accidentally.                                                         
   27. Keep decisions in a single canonical location. Scattered decisions are effectively lost decisions. One primary store, everything else points to it.                                              
   28. Separate durable decisions from transient status. Status churns, decisions persist. Blending them buries the durable content.                                                                    
   29. Avoid duplicating the same decision in multiple places. Copies diverge and the divergence becomes the record. Single source, many references.                                                    
   30. Record supersession explicitly. Never edit a decision to look like it was always different. Append the new decision and link the old one as superseded.                                          
   31. Don't record decisions in code comments. Comments belong to the code's durable why, not project bookkeeping. Keep decision tracking in the decision store where it's retrievable.                
   32. Keep the format plain and tool-agnostic. Markdown or equivalent survives tool churn. Proprietary formats outlive their usefulness.                                                               
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   Traceability & Linking (33–42)                                                                                                                                                                       
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   33. Link decisions to the work they affect. Files, modules, specs, tickets. An unlinked decision is a fact nobody will find at the point of use.                                                     
   34. Link decisions to the decisions they depend on. Build a dependency chain, not a flat list. Chains reveal what breaks when one decision reverses.                                                 
   35. Record the parent decision when splitting. Sub-decisions need lineage. Orphan sub-decisions get reversed without knowing what they supported.                                                    
   36. Point forward to consequences. Record what was changed as a result. Effect-tracking makes the record actionable.                                                                                 
   37. Link the follow-up actions. Every decision generates tasks; capture them with the decision. Otherwise the decision is made and never executed.                                                   
   38. Reference the trigger phrase for recovery. Attach searchable hooks so rg can find the entry later. Distinctive tokens beat generic headings.                                                     
   39. Cross-reference related sessions. Decisions rarely live in one sitting. Cross-session links prevent contradictory re-decisions.                                                                  
   40. Record the exact artifact versions in play. Commit SHAs, branch names, package versions. Later readers can't reproduce the context without them.                                                 
   41. Tag decisions by category. Architecture, process, security, tooling, product. Tags make bulk review tractable.                                                                                   
   42. Keep a top-level index. A flat list of decision titles plus links. The index is what makes the archive usable at scale.                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   Ownership, Review & Change (43–52)                                                                                                                                                                   
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   43. Assign a review date or trigger. Decisions without a revisit condition become permanent by accident. Set an explicit expiry or review event.                                                     
   44. Record who can reverse it. Authority matters when conditions change. Unclear authority means decisions linger past their usefulness.                                                             
   45. Review decisions at natural checkpoints. Phase boundaries, releases, incidents. Scheduled review beats reactive archaeology.                                                                     
   46. Mark decisions invalidated by new information. Invalidation is a decision too and needs its own entry. Silent invalidation leaves confusing ghosts.                                              
   47. Never delete a decision record. Only supersede or mark rejected. Deletion destroys the audit trail that made the record valuable.                                                                
   48. Record rejected decisions in place. A rejection is still a preserved answer. Moving it out of sight invites the same debate again.                                                               
   49. Note the cost of reversal. Cheap reversals don't need ceremony; expensive ones need more evidence. This calibrates how much rigor to apply.                                                      
   50. Keep a change log of the decision record itself. When entries are edited, say so. Untracked edits undermine trust in the whole record.                                                           
   51. Record disagreements that were resolved informally. The informal resolution is often the real decision. Undocumented agreements evaporate with the participants.                                 
   52. Escalate unresolved decisions rather than dropping them. An open decision left unowned will resurface as a surprise. Assign it or explicitly defer it with a date.                               
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   Session & Compaction Resilience (53–60)                                                                                                                                                              
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   53. Capture decisions before any compaction or handoff. Compaction is lossy and decisions are the highest-value tokens. Write them out while reasoning is still present.                             
   54. Anchor the session with a distinctive marker. A unique token makes recovery deterministic rather than probabilistic. Record the marker alongside the decisions it labels.                        
   55. Store the marker in a recoverable place. Not just in the conversation window. Anything that must survive must live outside the volatile store.                                                   
   56. Summarize decisions into a resumable packet. Decisions, rationale, open questions, next actions. The packet should let a fresh session continue without re-reading everything.                   
   57. Verify recovery by reading back. After resuming, state the recovered decisions before acting. Silent recovery of wrong decisions is worse than none.                                             
   58. Separate what's decided from what's presumed. Presumptions masquerade as decisions after a gap. Explicit labeling keeps resumption honest.                                                       
   59. Keep unresolved items in an explicit parking lot. Unfinished thinking needs a named container. Otherwise it gets relabeled as a decision on recovery.                                            
   60. Recite the marker at the end of every preservation pass. It closes the loop and confirms the record's anchor is intact. Verify it once more before declaring the session preserved.
