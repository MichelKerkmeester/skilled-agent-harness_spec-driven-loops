   33. Link decisions to the work they affect. Files, modules, specs, tickets. An unlinked decision is a fact nobody will find at the point of use.                                                     
   34. Link decisions to the decisions they depend on. Build a dependency chain, not a flat list. Chains reveal what breaks when one decision reverses.                                                 
   35. Record the parent decision when splitting. Sub-decisions need lineage. Orphan sub-decisions get reversed without knowing what they supported.                                                    
   36. Point forward to consequences. Record what was changed as a result. Effect-tracking makes the record actionable.                                                                                 
   37. Link the follow-up actions. Every decision generates tasks; capture them with the decision. Otherwise the decision is made and never executed.                                                   
   38. Reference the trigger phrase for recovery. Attach searchable hooks so rg can find the entry later. Distinctive tokens beat generic headings.                                                     
   39. Cross-reference related sessions. Decisions rarely live in one sitting. Cross-session links prevent contradictory re-decisions.                                                                  
   40. Record the exact artifact versions in play. Commit SHAs, branch names, package versions. Later readers can't reproduce the context without them.                                                 
   41. Tag decisions by category. Architecture, process, security, tooling, product. Tags make bulk review tractable.                                                                                   
   42. Keep a top-level index. A flat list of decision titles plus links. The index is what makes the archive usable at scale.                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   Ownership, Review & Change (43–52)                                                                                                                                                                   
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   43. Assign a review date or trigger. Decisions without a revisit condition become permanent by accident. Set an explicit expiry or review event.                                                     
   44. Record who can reverse it. Authority matters when conditions change. Unclear authority means decisions linger past their usefulness.                                                             
   45. Review decisions at natural checkpoints. Phase boundaries, releases, incidents. Scheduled review beats reactive archaeology.                                                                     
   46. Mark decisions invalidated by new information. Invalidation is a decision too and needs its own entry. Silent invalidation leaves confusing ghosts.                                              
   47. Never delete a decision record. Only supersede or mark rejected. Deletion destroys the audit trail that made the record valuable.                                                                
   48. Record rejected decisions in place. A rejection is still a preserved answer. Moving it out of sight invites the same debate again.                                                               
   49. Note the cost of reversal. Cheap reversals don't need ceremony; expensive ones need more evidence. This calibrates how much rigor to apply.                                                      
   50. Keep a change log of the decision record itself. When entries are edited, say so. Untracked edits undermine trust in the whole record.                                                           
   51. Record disagreements that were resolved informally. The informal resolution is often the real decision. Undocumented agreements evaporate with the participants.                                 
   52. Escalate unresolved decisions rather than dropping them. An open decision left unowned will resurface as a surprise. Assign it or explicitly defer it with a date.                               
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   Session & Compaction Resilience (53–60)                                                                                                                                                              
                                                                                                                                                                                                        
   53. Capture decisions before any compaction or handoff. Compaction is lossy and decisions are the highest-value tokens. Write them out while reasoning is still present.                             
   54. Anchor the session with a distinctive marker. A unique token makes recovery deterministic rather than probabilistic. Record the marker alongside the decisions it labels.                        
   55. Store the marker in a recoverable place. Not just in the conversation window. Anything that must survive must live outside the volatile store.                                                   
   56. Summarize decisions into a resumable packet. Decisions, rationale, open questions, next actions. The packet should let a fresh session continue without re-reading everything.                   
   57. Verify recovery by reading back. After resuming, state the recovered decisions before acting. Silent recovery of wrong decisions is worse than none.                                             
   58. Separate what's decided from what's presumed. Presumptions masquerade as decisions after a gap. Explicit labeling keeps resumption honest.                                                       
   59. Keep unresolved items in an explicit parking lot. Unfinished thinking needs a named container. Otherwise it gets relabeled as a decision on recovery.                                            
   60. Recite the marker at the end of every preservation pass. It closes the loop and confirms the record's anchor is intact. Verify it once more before declaring the session preserved.
