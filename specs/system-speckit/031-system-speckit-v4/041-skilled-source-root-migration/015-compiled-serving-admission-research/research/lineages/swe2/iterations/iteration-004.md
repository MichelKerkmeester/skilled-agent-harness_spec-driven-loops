4. **Restore's biggest risk is re-buying what retirement sold.** The full lane was 268 files (49 scripts + ~119 playbook/asset docs + 10 command surfaces + 10 feature-catalog + tests). A surgical restore avoids most of that, but it re-owns the frozen-scorer freeze discipline (the pins exist so the comparator can't drift mid-run — a restored trio needs the same digests or a decided re-freeze), and `router-replay` remains a *model* of the prose router (substring-scoring over the SKILL.md dicts), not the router itself — parity was always compiled-vs-replay-proxy, and the spec's own bar ("matches its legacy decision") inherits that proxy gap. [SOURCE: commit:b45ea54cea3 census; file:router-replay.cjs@b45ea54cea3^:7-21]

5. **Path B (new checker) has the smallest build surface — ~500-800 lines on live deps.** Components: (a) corpus — either a slim playbook-gold loader for the two authored shapes (~300 lines, still needs `@spec-kit/shared`, live) OR the per-hub `fixtures/canary-cases.v1.json` already normalized as JSON with `expectedAction`/`expectedSelectionKind`/`expectedModes`/`gold.{expectedIntents,expectedResources}` — 67 cases across the 5 hubs (route 50, defer 7, reject 7, clarify 3), all inside the serving closure (zero loader cost); (b) comparator — `workflowMode` set equality + leaf must-include + `selectionKind`/ordered-target projection + gold-parse-failure-as-violation (~200 lines, a port of `evaluateRouteGold` + the parity projection); (c) rollup/report (~150 lines). No retired-module maintenance, no frozen-trio pins needed. [SOURCE: file:.skilled/bin/lib/compiled-routing/009-parent-hub-rollout/*/fixtures/canary-cases.v1.json (per-hub census: 10/9/14/13/21 cases); file:score-skill-benchmark.cjs@b45ea54cea3^:891-945]

6. **The canary-cases shortcut has a self-grading hazard.** The fixture is authored by the same shadow-child build it validates — a hub can tune engine and cases together. The playbook corpus is the independent authored live-execution contract (Lane C deliberately used it, not the fixtures). A credible checker can use canary-cases as the engine smoke gate but should keep playbook gold (or an equivalently independent corpus) as the admission bar. [SOURCE: file:compiled-routing-architecture.md:55 (fixtures are the hub's own scenario set); file:load-playbook-scenarios.cjs@b45ea54cea3^:7-24 ("far better benchmark corpus than synthetic decontaminated fixtures")]

7. **Path B's deepest risk is semantic, not technical.** Gold-checking changes what the bar *means*: it admits a hub whose compiled decisions match gold even where they differ from legacy — serving could then change user-visible routing on ungolded inputs, which parity's equivalence guarantee excluded. Conversely it blocks a hub where compiled==legacy but both share a pre-existing gold gap (the CS-002/CS-005 pattern). Whether the tighter-but-different bar is acceptable is an admission-policy call the spec's operators must make — it cannot be inherited from Lane C. [SOURCE: file:compiled-routing-parity.cjs@b45ea54cea3^:712-740; file:.skilled/skills/sk-code/benchmark/reports/compiled-routing/2026-07-21--playbook-verify--sonnet/report.md:33-44]

8. **Path C (keep closed) is near-zero cost now, and its blast radius is smaller than it first looks.** The five admitted hubs keep serving (guard: all fresh; authored==runtime manifests identical). Policy *evolution* of admitted hubs still works — `refresh` re-mints to the new generation preserving `servingAuthority`, and the auto-remint hook stages both manifests — because the parity bar was per-hub admission, not per-policy. What closes forever: NEW hub admission (the `compiled-serving` verdict is unreachable per the architecture doc's step 2), and any *re-verification* of an admitted hub's drift (nothing re-measures parity; a rebuilt engine serving stale-by-design would drift silently past the mechanical refresh). [SOURCE: file:.skilled/bin/lib/compiled-route-manifest.cjs:693-780 (refresh preserves servingAuthority); file:.opencode/scripts/git-hooks/pre-commit:281-330; file:compiled-routing-architecture.md:76-83]

9. **Keep-closed also accrues doc debt and semantic drift.** Docs and comments still say "seven hubs" (reality: five after `f931ba2e14` and `a9286399bc3`); the spec itself repeats "the seven hubs already admitted". The longer admission stays closed the more the docs' admission story diverges from the fleet. Closing permanently also strands `create-skill --compiled-routing ready` — it keeps minting inert manifests for a verdict that can never be earned. [SOURCE: file:compiled-routing-architecture.md:34-42,87-105; commit:f931ba2e14; commit:a9286399bc3; file:resolve.cjs:29-40]

10. **Comparative ledger.** Restore: ~3,700-line surgical landing (5-7 files) or ~268-file full lane; medium-high build cost; highest fidelity to the original bar (still compiled-vs-replay); risks: freeze-discipline re-owning, path-churn verification, proxy gap. New checker: ~500-800 lines; lowest build cost; runs on live deps only; risks: semantic bar change, self-grading if fixture-only, policy decisions needed on defer/holdout/negative and on shared-gold-gap handling. Keep closed: ~0 build cost; risks: permanent admission block, silent per-policy drift on admitted hubs, compounding doc debt, stranded `ready` mints. [SOURCE: synthesis of findings 2-9]
