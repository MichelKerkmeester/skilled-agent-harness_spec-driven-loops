## 7. WHAT WORKED
- reading the *use site* before the definition. The arithmetic the dispatch asked about is a real defect, but it sits on a block nothing executes, and only the repo-wide `rg` for the key showed that; reading gate blocks alone would have produced a confident, incomplete answer. (iteration 1)
- asking what the *skip rule does not cover*. The dispatch framed lead 3 as "the review is skipped, so it guards nothing"; the durable defect is the opposite direction — blocks that stop for a user in a mode defined by the absence of one. Reading the mode contract first made the asymmetry visible. (iteration 2)
- separating the structural tables from the behavioural blocks before claiming drift. Lead 5 predicted drift everywhere; the tables refuted it and the blocks confirmed it, and that split is what makes the finding actionable — you reconcile behaviour, not structure. (iteration 3)
- looking for a *consumer* rather than a *definition*. Every lead so far described something declared; the sweep's question — who reads this key — produced the class that explains four of the earlier findings and found `circuit_breaker` in the same state. (iteration 4)
- ranking by damage-today rather than damage-as-written. It reordered the dispatch's own leads — the `plan_clarity` arithmetic fell to rank 2 as "latent under a dead block", while the auto-mode STOPs, which no lead named, took rank 1. (iteration 5)

## 8. WHAT FAILED
- an initial pass assumed the gates were invoked by step name and searched for `speckit-implement step 5 gate`-style wording. The files have no such back-reference, so the search returned nothing and looked like a failed query rather than a negative result. (iteration 1)
- an early pass counted `applies_to` hits per file and nearly reported "32 skipped checkpoints" as the finding. That number describes intent, not damage; the damage needed the neighbouring option lists. (iteration 2)
- counting `[DEVIATION:` files first and treating the result as the emission rate understated the real channel by 15x. The summary section was in the same file family and only showed up when I asked what else records a deviation. (iteration 3)
- an initial pass compared the three `quality_gates` blocks field by field, which produced a long list of differences and no damage estimate. The comparison only became useful after asking which copy is invoked. (iteration 4)
- the first ranking attempt sorted by file and section, which produced a tidy list with no priority — every item looked equally urgent. The useful sort key was "what can a reader do after this defect" rather than "where is it". (iteration 5)
