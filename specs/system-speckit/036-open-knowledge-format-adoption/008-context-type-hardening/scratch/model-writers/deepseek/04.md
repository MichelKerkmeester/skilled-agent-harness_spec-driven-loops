# Document an Incident Review

## 1. Overview

Provide a repeatable way to document an incident review as a durable artifact: a single document that records what happened, why, how it was resolved and what changes will prevent recurrence. The output is written documentation, not remediation work.

## 2. Problem

Incident reviews are often captured as scattered chat threads or memory, losing the timeline, the evidence and the follow-up actions. Without a structured document, the same failure mode recurs and accountability for fixes is unclear.

## 3. Goals

- Capture a complete, factual incident narrative in a standard structure.
- Distinguish confirmed facts from hypotheses and unknowns.
- Record follow-up actions with owners and due dates.
- Produce a review that can be shared with stakeholders without rewriting.

## 4. Non-Goals

- Performing the incident remediation itself.
- Building automated incident tooling or dashboards.
- Assigning blame or conducting disciplinary review.

## 5. Functional Requirements

1. **Metadata** — Record incident id, title, severity, status, detection and resolution timestamps, and author.
2. **Summary** — One paragraph a non-participant can read to understand impact and outcome.
3. **Timeline** — Chronological, timestamped events from first signal through resolution, each with a source or evidence link.
4. **Impact** — Affected systems, users, duration, and quantified business or data impact where known.
5. **Root Cause** — The causal chain to the trigger; contributing factors listed separately.
6. **Detection & Response** — How the incident was found and how it was handled; what worked and what hindered.
7. **Follow-Up Actions** — Each action with owner, priority and due date; link to tracked work items where they exist.
8. **Lessons Learned** — What would have prevented or shortened the incident.
9. **Confidence Labels** — Mark each finding as confirmed, inferred or unknown.

## 6. Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Document follows the section structure above, with no section left silently empty (empty sections state "none identified").
- [ ] Every timeline entry carries a timestamp and an evidence reference or an explicit "unverified" marker.
- [ ] Root cause and contributing factors are stated separately.
- [ ] Every follow-up action has an owner and a due date.
- [ ] Hypotheses are labeled and not presented as established fact.
- [ ] Document is self-contained and shareable without the original incident channel.

## 7. Approach

Draft from incident channel history, alerts and logs; confirm the timeline with responders; classify findings by confidence; review the draft with incident participants before publishing; store the final document alongside the incident record and link it from follow-up work items.

## 8. Risks and Mitigations

- **Incomplete timeline** — corroborate against alert and log timestamps.
- **Hindsight bias** — record decisions as they were made with available information.
- **Unassigned actions** — block publication until every action has an owner.
- **Sensitive data exposure** — redact credentials, personal data and customer identifiers before publishing.

## 9. Out of Scope

Remediation of identified issues, changes to monitoring or alerting, and process changes beyond what the review document records as recommendations.
