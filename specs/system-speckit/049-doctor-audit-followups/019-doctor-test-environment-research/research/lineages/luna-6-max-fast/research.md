## 1. `/doctor:speckit`: make phrase quality advisory

The current retrieval doctor maps any nonzero phrase-quality bucket to medium `corpus_pollution`, reports that class as staleness, includes it in `severity_max`, and recommends corpus cleanup because the phrases supposedly “never rank.” As the checked-in corpus contains nonzero buckets, this makes a clean `STATUS=OK` unreachable even when the index is fresh. [SOURCE: `.skilled/commands/doctor/assets/doctor-speckit-retrieval.yaml:135-137,191-205,211-215`]

The diagnostics count 251 flagged phrases against 33,719 total, about 0.74%. That is useful hygiene data, not evidence that those phrases harm retrieval. The scorer gives exact matches 1.0 before its short-phrase guard; non-exact phrases under two tokens do not match, while containment and sufficiently covered token overlap can score. The lookup ranks scored phrases, keeps each document’s best match, and does not filter by `phraseQuality`; the generator records quality classes but does not remove those phrases. The blanket “never rank” rationale is therefore incorrect. [SOURCE: `.skilled/skills/system-spec-kit/runtime/cli/retrieval/fixtures/generation-diagnostics.json:32-51`] [SOURCE: `.skilled/skills/system-spec-kit/runtime/cli/retrieval/lib/normalize.mjs:120-150`] [SOURCE: `.skilled/skills/system-spec-kit/runtime/cli/retrieval/lookup-trigger-index.mjs:171-210`] [SOURCE: `.skilled/skills/system-spec-kit/runtime/cli/retrieval/generate-trigger-index.mjs:253-296`]

**Recommendation:** preserve phrase-quality counts and share in a diagnostic advisory section, but remove `corpus_pollution` from staleness classes and `severity_max`. Do not add an arbitrary percentage threshold without retrieval-precision evidence. Do not bulk-clean phrases or filter them in the generator merely to turn status green; make selective source cleanup only when a measured query demonstrates a problem. Leave retrieval ranking unchanged.
