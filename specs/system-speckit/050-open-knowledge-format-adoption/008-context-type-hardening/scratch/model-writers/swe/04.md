# Document an Incident Review

## Overview

Create a durable, blameless record of a resolved incident so the team can
understand what happened, why it happened, and what will be done to prevent
recurrence. The document is written for future readers who were not involved
in the incident.

## Goal

Deliver a single incident review document that is factual, complete, and
actionable, with every follow-up item assigned an owner and a due date.

## Scope

### In scope

- One incident review document for a single resolved incident
- Incident metadata: identifier, severity, status, date(s), author(s), reviewer(s)
- Summary: 2-3 sentence description of the incident and its user-facing impact
- Impact: who/what was affected, duration, and quantified blast radius where known
- Timeline: chronological list of key events (detection, escalation, mitigation,
  resolution) with timestamps in one stated timezone
- Root cause: the contributing factors traced to their source, not the symptom
- Response assessment: what went well, what went poorly, what was lucky
- Action items: preventive and corrective follow-ups, each with a named owner
  and a due date
- Lessons learned: what this incident revealed about systems, process, or tooling

### Out of scope

- Executing the action items themselves
- Changing incident-response tooling, alerting, or runbooks
- Assigning blame or disciplinary conclusions
- Reviewing incidents that are still open or under active mitigation

## Requirements

1. The document is written in blameless language: describe systems and
   decisions, not personal fault.
2. Every factual claim is verifiable from logs, monitoring, chat records, or
   ticket history; mark anything unverifiable as UNKNOWN rather than guessing.
3. The timeline is chronological and internally consistent.
4. Root cause analysis goes past the proximate trigger to the contributing
   conditions that allowed it.
5. Every action item has exactly one accountable owner and a due date.
6. No secrets, credentials, or customer-identifying data beyond what the
   incident record requires.
7. The document states where it lives (path/ticket link) so it can be found later.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] All in-scope sections are present and non-empty
- [ ] Timeline events carry timestamps in a single stated timezone
- [ ] Root cause section identifies at least one contributing condition, not
      only the triggering event
- [ ] Every action item has an owner and a due date
- [ ] No blame language, secrets, or unverifiable claims presented as fact
- [ ] A reviewer who was not the author confirms the document is
      understandable without incident context

## Verification

- Read the finished document end to end and check each acceptance criterion
  against it explicitly.
- Cross-check two or more timeline entries against their underlying sources
  (logs, tickets, chat) to confirm accuracy.
- Confirm the document is stored at its stated location and linked from the
  incident ticket.
