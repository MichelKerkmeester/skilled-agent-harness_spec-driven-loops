# Reference Evidence for the OpenBot Parity Build: Synthesis

Final synthesis of the reference-evidence fan-out. It covers four lineages of 10 iterations each: `vibe-1` and `vibe-2` on `cli-mistral-vibe` with `deepseek-flash`, and `pi-1` and `pi-2` on `cli-pi` with `deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017` at max thinking. That is all 40 iteration records and all 303 findings in the merged registry. Every finding was then re-checked against the line it cites before it was allowed into this document.

## 1. How to Read This

**Question.** For each of the fourteen phases of `specs/system-skilled-bots/003-openbot-parity-build/`, which reference code should a builder lift or mirror, where does the plan differ from the references, and which failures did the references hit that the plan does not guard against? And what do the ten reference repos have that neither the plan nor the skip list covers?

Any other path is written in full from the repository root. S1, S2 and S3 are the three earlier syntheses of this research parent: what to build, the plan review and the plan-depth review.

**Grades.** Every row carries one of two grades. `confirmed` means the cited lines were read and the claim would have read differently if it were false. `weak` means the citation resolves but supports only part of the claim, or the claim is an inference the lines do not state. The row is then written down to what the lines support. A finding graded `wrong` or `not verified` is not in this document. It is in `research/regrade-log.md` with the reason.

**Origin ids.** `V1-3-2` is lineage vibe-1, iteration 3, finding 2. `V2`, `P1` and `P2` are vibe-2, pi-1 and pi-2. `S` marks a row the verifier found while checking a lineage finding. No lineage reported it.

**Licences.** The build is MIT. A source under MIT or Apache-2.0 may be lifted with its notice. A source under GPL-3.0 is a design reference only, and its rows say "mirror the design, copy no code". Every row names the licence and the line it was read from.

**Absences.** A statement that the plan lacks something is always written as a search: the patterns searched and where. It is never a certainty.

**How the findings were verified.** Seven verification workers on Claude Opus, one per phase group, opened every cited location, graded each finding, searched the plan for a guard that already covers it and searched S1, S2 and S3 for an earlier copy. The brief they worked from is `scratch/synthesis-inputs/verify-brief.md`, and their drafts are beside it. The sections below are those drafts in phase order. The orchestrator read every table, opened 20 of the load-bearing citations by hand and reran four counts. Section 9 lists what was hand-checked and what rests on the workers alone.

## 2. Bottom Line

**The answer.** The plan already carries most of what the ten references offer. Of 303 raw findings, 121 rows survive verification: 90 confirmed and 31 weak. The regrade log holds 332 rows for the rest: 106 wrong, 112 out of scope, 43 downgraded to weak, 38 with a corrected citation, 32 already carried by an earlier synthesis and 1 not verified. So the references are not a store of missing features. What a builder gains is a short list of lifts, about twenty guards the plan lacks, six places where the plan contradicts itself, five corrections to the earlier syntheses and a licence map that rules most of the new material out as code.

**The licence map decides what can be copied.** `openhuman-full` and every one of its 21 vendored crates are GPL-3.0, and so is `tinychannels-main`. Those two are the largest references for the terminal screen, security policy and messaging. They are design references only. The code that can be lifted is small and comes from OpenBot and OpenGhost (MIT) and from rakazo, turnstone and buzz (Apache-2.0).

**Lifts worth taking.**

1. 002: OpenBot's `FIRING_FRAME`, three fixed sentences that tell a scheduled turn it is a firing. OpenBot wrote it after a live routine did nothing and recorded success. The plan has no such framing.
2. 009: the two OpenGhost lines that name a skill's directory, so a relative link in a skill body resolves against the right folder.
3. 008: rakazo's secret matcher as a whole file, with its state cap and its per-value fallback. The plan's one-line description drops both.
4. 004: buzz's `workspace_section`, a prompt-level statement of the working directory with exact-bytes tests. It is a reference for a phase the plan-depth synthesis said had none.

**Guards with the highest stakes.** The order is judgment.

1. 003 and 010: the plan never turns on bracketed paste. A pasted `y` plus newline can answer an item nobody named, and a three-line paste into `bots chat` starts three runs.
2. 011: an error on a send that may have reached the platform must be recorded unknown before it is treated as retryable. The plan names no order, so a retry on chunk 2 resends chunk 1.
3. 008: the leak scan misses base64 without padding, URL-safe base64 and the lowercase hex form the vault itself stores. The placeholder that AC-010 tests is not in rakazo's set, and rakazo's guard has a dev gate that Vitest opens. The `secret-store-unreadable` detail has no rule against carrying captured program output.
4. 006: after a deny, the resumed prompt says only to continue. OpenBot tells the model not to reach the same result another way. The next packet's rules store is keyed "bot plus kind", where the references key on bot, tool, place and account.
5. 009: the shared label tells the model a skill body is loaded "for reference, not as instructions", which works against the phase's purpose. Nothing refuses invisible or bidi code points in a body, and `.skilled/skills/local-webflow` is a symlink to a folder outside the tree.
6. 011: `conformance-rules.md` describes a synthetic transport that passes two of the five rules for any adapter. A webhook set while `listen` runs is never cleared. Slack's section cap of 3,000 characters and label cap of 75 are not checked.
7. 002: a routine that only tells may fire 96 times a day under the 15-minute floor, where the reference allows a check-in once an hour.
8. 014: the rule that a regression test must fail without the fix has no ledger column, so nothing records that it was checked.

**Where the plan contradicts itself.** 005 `spec.md:96` against REQ-010 on what `memory: false` withholds. 009 REQ-003 and its fixture design against decision D11 on a bot at both caps. 011 `conformance-rules.md:33` against child 007's plan on the conformance fixture. 011's Telegram row against its WhatsApp row on a `5xx`. 003 `component-inventory.md:79`, which cites two sources for a sanitizer rule that neither implements. 006, which calls its kind order OpenBot's although no OpenBot line states a precedence.

**Corrections to the earlier syntheses.** S3 says no context repository applies to 004, and buzz does. S3 names `shared/tool-name.ts` as the classifier for 006, and no classifier exists in the snapshot. S3 recommended a positional index for `--forget`, and both references that have a forget use a stable id. S3 says no reference exists for 007, and OpenBot's app-grant types are one. S1's licence table omits tinychannels, which is GPL-3.0-only. The spec of this packet names five vendored crates in `openhuman-full`. There are 21.

**Net-new.** Sixteen candidates survive, ranked in section 5. The first three are a per-bot cap on runs per hour, inbound coalescing with one recorded outcome per message and a stall watchdog. Nothing found contradicts the skip list.

**What would change this answer.** The grades rest on one verification pass per phase group and a hand check of 20 citations. A second verifier could regrade a row. Three claims rest on inference and say so in their rows: that models skip a skill under the current label, that the bots CLI dies on a closed pipe and that a shared memory can leak a private fact into a wider chat. The crossterm behaviour behind the paste finding is cited from the crate's registry source, which is outside this repository. The orchestrator opened both cited places in the local cargo registry copy of crossterm 0.29.0 and they say what the row claims, but the citation checker cannot follow them.

### Ranked order

The order is the orchestrator's judgment over the verified rows above. It weighs three things: who notices the absence and how soon, how small a file-backed version is and whether the reference may be lifted or only mirrored. It follows the verifier's proposed order with two changes. Candidate 11 moves up, because three phase sections ask for the same next-step text independently (005 pitfall 1, 008 pitfall 4 and the refusals 007 already writes). Candidate 7 moves above candidate 6, because 009 needs it to test its own label wording. The numbers in brackets are the row numbers of the candidate table.

1. Per-bot hourly run cap (1). The smallest guard against the most likely cost surprise. It applies to every trigger the plan already has.
2. Inbound coalescing and one outcome per message (2). The first group chat after 011 meets it. The dedupe half is already planned, so only the folding, the outcome field and the cap are new.
3. Stall watchdog (3). One timer and one cause. It turns a silent 15-minute hold into a named row.
4. Next-step text on refusals (11). A table of sentences, MIT as a pattern, and wanted by three phases.
5. Silent health probe (4). MIT and small. It reuses the routine layer and gives an unattended host a signal it lacks today.
6. Pattern redaction on outgoing text (5). The leak class 008 does not catch, reachable once 011 sends text to chats. GPL design only.
7. Live skill-adherence check (7). Apache-2.0 case shape. It needs a tool-call trace the runtime does not record yet.
8. Reader-set labels (6). The only guard against an audience leak, Apache-2.0, but it needs a label on memory and conversations. Weak, because the leak path is inferred.
9. What-if read (8). MIT and read-only. It pays off once 006 to 008 have deciders to run.
10. Summary beyond the conversation window (9). Felt in long chats only, and 010 already names compaction as a later packet.
11. Long-ask acknowledgement and a routine-to-conversation link (10). Weak, because the plan already returns the result at run end.
12. Tool-name wording (12). Polish, one pure function.
13. Document output (13). Rarely asked for by a terminal-first team, and GPL design only.
14. Egress URL policy (14). The runtime's own outbound surface is small, and a child's traffic is outside its reach.
15. Trace replay checker (15). Built for a multi-tenant relay the skip list excludes.
16. Package manifest (16). Needed only for cross-repository distribution, and half of it waits on deferred hooks.

**What would change the order.** A team that never links a bot to a group chat can drop candidates 2 and 8. A team that pays a flat rate cares less about candidate 1. None of the sixteen is needed for parity with what the plan already promises.
