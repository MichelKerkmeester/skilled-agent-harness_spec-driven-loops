Four decisions a reader can misread were implicit in the paint code. Each is now declared beside
the data, held by a check, and proved against a mutated copy.

## What Changed

#### New Features

- **`MARKS`.** Every form declares what its marks do: `points` (`none`, `sparse`, `all`), `fill` (`none`, `gradient`, `flat`) and `zero` (`baseline`, `meaningful`), with one line of why. Held by `mark-policy`.
- **Key kinds.** Every declared series carries `indicator`, `swatch` or `rule`, and both the legend chip and the card row draw the kind the series names. A stroked series no longer reads as one more bar. Held by `tooltip-indicator`.
- **`REFERENCE`.** Every form built on one vertical value scale and a drawn grid declares a list of `{ value, label, why }` lines drawn across the plot and named at the right edge. `daily-line` ships one at the first week's average and the `orders-after-the-price-change` delivery one at the level before the change; both are the level their headline claims. Held by `reference-line`.
- **Cursor guide.** A hairline at the hovered reading on the forms that earn it, declared as `guide` in the `MARKS` block and permitted only with two or more series or more than twenty readings. Held by `cursor-guide`.

#### Fixes

- **A multi-series card painted every row in the hovered series' colour.** `bar-line-composed` and `population-pyramid` told a reader that both figures belonged to the mark under the pointer when one belonged to the other measure. A card row now carries its own series.

- **The marked low's value in the orders delivery** was printed beside its dot at the dot's own height and read as part of the mark. It now sits under the mark, where the template it was built from puts it.

#### Notes

- The planned consumer for `REFERENCE` was `bullet`, and it turned out not to be one: its target is per row against four different scales, which a chart-wide list cannot express. It stays in its data, and `daily-line` is the worked case instead.
- The vocabulary for key kinds is two, not three. A card whose rows are statistics of one series already carries `data-single-series` in its markup and draws no key, so a `none` kind would have been a second way to say what the markup says.
- `stacked-area` clears the guide's density bar four times over and declares `false`: its card opens on a band rather than a column, so a hairline would sit at the middle of the band every time. Density is necessary and not sufficient, and the check cannot see the rest.

#### Review

An Opus review on a second account, plus a parallel audit here, found ten assertions that passed for an incidental reason rather than for the reason their message gave. Every one was reproduced on a mutated copy before the fix and again after it.

- A helper's own definition satisfied the check that it was called, in two families.
- The key declaration and the series count were read from the whole script rather than from the series block, so an unrelated object literal could stand in for a missing declaration and three unrelated key literals could fail a correct file.
- A key could be painted around the declaration by calling the helper twice for one of two keys.
- The `MARKS` block was read without stripping comments, so a commented-out declaration still answered for the file.
- `gradient` and `flat` were interchangeable, and `points` and `zero` were spell-checked and never held against the drawing.
- The reference drawing was proved to read the list but not to put a rule in the document, and the rule's own style was not required, so a styleless line rendered invisible.
- The guide element was proved to exist but not to be appended.
- The corpus-wide colour rule had been widened to let the mark policy's paint words through, which let any mark take a stroke of `flat`. The exemption now cuts the declaration block instead of the vocabulary.

The same review found the commit's own card-colour fix wrong: it tagged each row with an ordinal where two forms number their marks and their tokens differently, turning one wrong colour into two. A card row now carries its series' own token, which also fixed a card that had always painted every status the same colour.
