A second Style Reference sits beside the stock one: evilcharts, written from that library's own
stylesheet, so the corpus can be themed to it through the override path.

## What Changed

#### New Features

- **`assets/style-reference/evilcharts/`.** A v3 Style Reference read out of the evilcharts source: eighteen colour tokens converted from the `oklch()` values its stylesheet declares, the three typefaces it binds, and the radius ladder derived from its `--radius: 0.525rem`. An `origin.md` separates what was measured from what was authored.
- **A `tokens.json` beside it.** The applicator reads `darkMode` from a sidecar under exactly that name. evilcharts has a dark theme, so declaring it is what makes the dark chart set resolve against evilcharts' own `#090909` rather than the corpus stock dark chrome.

#### Notes

- **Both chart sets are in the colour table, because both are tokens the reference ships.** Measured against the two grounds at the 3:1 a mark needs, four of the nine hues clear on both — Flame 3.60/5.53, Teal 3.67/5.43, Violet 4.12/4.83, Rose 3.75/5.30. Deep and Cobalt clear only on white; Gold, Amber and Jade only on ink. A light-only table was refused, not for want of hues that clear on ink but because the applicator picks series greedily for hue separation as well as contrast, and the light set could not offer it four separated candidates on the dark ground.

  *Corrected in v1.12.0.0.* The line above originally said the two sets were complements and named
  the wrong hues on each side. It was wrong about three of the nine, and wrong about the reason.
- **Two of the five light chart colours cannot carry a thin stroke.** Gold and Amber reach 1.72:1 and 2.13:1 against evilcharts' own white, below the 3:1 a mark needs. That is recorded in the reference rather than corrected, because a Style Reference documents a source.
- Applying it themes 25 of the 29 forms; the four ordered ramps are refused by design, as with any reference.
