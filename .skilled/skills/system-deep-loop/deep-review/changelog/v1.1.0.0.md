`deep-review` now makes its review-packet expected behavior explicit across naming, lifecycle, reducer model and runtime mirrors.

## Why This Release

The goal is to make the workflow easier to follow and easier to trust.

---

## What's New at a Glance

- **The review-packet contract is explicit.** It covers artifacts, lifecycle, release-readiness terms and reducer expectations.
- **Runtime and workflow surfaces match the review model.** The runtime mirrors point to one packet model, and the auto and confirm assets carry the workflow expectations.
- **Review guidance follows one lifecycle and state model.** The references, manual testing playbook and config asset reflect the updated expectations.

---

## Review Contract

#### Canonical Artifacts Are Frozen

The canonical review artifacts are `deep-review-config.json`, `deep-review-state.jsonl`, `deep-review-findings-registry.json`, `.deep-review-pause` and `deep-review-dashboard.md`. They are frozen across the skill, references, workflow assets and runtime mirrors.

&nbsp;

#### Review Naming Is Consistent

The main naming is `deep-review-*` across all surfaces. Legacy `deep-research-*` names are limited to the intentional scratch migration path rather than live review surfaces.

&nbsp;

#### Lifecycle and Reducer Expectations

Lifecycle branches are explicit and consistent across the review packet: `resume`, `restart`, `fork` and `completed-continue`. Release-readiness states are first-class expected behavior terms rather than scattered prose. The expected behavior surfaces document reducer inputs, outputs and metrics directly, along with machine-owned boundaries.

---

## Runtime and Workflows

#### Mirrors and Workflows Align

OpenCode, Claude and Codex runtime mirrors all point to the same review packet model. The auto and confirm workflow assets encode migration logic, reducer refresh behavior, synthesis boundaries and release-readiness propagation.

---

## Review Guidance

#### References and Operator Guidance

The review references describe one main lifecycle and one main state model. The manual testing playbook no longer teaches stale `deep-research-*` review-mode names. The config asset now exposes the reducer metrics expected by the expected behavior tests, making future drift easier to catch.
