# Anti-Slop Design Principles

A common language for spotting and naming generic AI-default visual decisions, so an extracted Style Reference records what makes a surface distinctive rather than flattening it to a median default.

---

## 1. OVERVIEW

### Purpose

Give the extraction a shared vocabulary for telling deliberate design apart from median web defaults. When a captured surface is genuinely distinctive, the reference should say so and preserve the move; when a surface is itself generic, that is worth naming too.

### Usage

Cite these principles when reading a surface's direction and when judging whether its tokens are purposeful.

---

## 2. PRINCIPLE SET

1. **Ground before styling.** A distinctive surface names its subject, audience, and one job before choosing color, type, layout, or motion. Look for that grounding in what you capture.
2. **Spend the free axis deliberately.** Where a design was not constrained on an axis, distinctive work makes a specific choice instead of the median web default.
3. **One memorable move beats scattered decoration.** A strong surface has a signature element and lets supporting details stay quiet.
4. **Tokens must explain themselves.** A palette, type scale, spacing rhythm, or motion curve should map to the product's purpose, not to a fashionable preset.
5. **Ornamental numbering is a smell.** `01 / 02 / 03`, fake process markers, and section chrome signal decoration unless the content is genuinely sequential.
6. **Affordance before novelty.** Distinctive design still needs clear hierarchy, visible focus, legible contrast, and predictable interaction targets.
7. **Category default, then deviation.** Name the common visual trope for the product category, then see what the surface kept, bent, or rejected.
8. **Restraint is visible.** If every element competes for attention, the surface has no focal point — record that as a finding, not a feature.

---

## 3. COMMON AI-DEFAULT SMELLS

- Generic cream background, editorial serif headline, terracotta accent, and soft card shadows without a subject-specific reason.
- Near-black page with one acid accent and oversized geometric gradients as the only identity.
- Hairline-heavy broadsheet layout where the content has no editorial or archival premise.
- Repeated glassmorphism, blur, glow, or mesh gradients used as decoration rather than structure.
- Motion on many small parts instead of one choreographed moment.

---

## 4. HOW THIS SKILL USES IT

Use these principles while reading an extracted surface's direction and while judging whether its captured tokens are purposeful. The goal is a Style Reference that preserves a surface's real intent — or honestly names it as generic — rather than smoothing every capture toward the same default.
