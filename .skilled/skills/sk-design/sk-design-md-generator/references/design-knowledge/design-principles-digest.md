# Design Principles Digest

A condensed read of what deliberate design looks like on a surface. This skill extracts existing designs rather than authoring new UI, so the value here is recognition: knowing the marks of intent versus the marks of a templated default, so a captured Style Reference preserves what makes a surface itself instead of smoothing it toward a generic average.

---

## 1. GROUND IT IN THE SUBJECT

Distinctiveness is found, not invented — it comes from the subject's own world: the materials, tools, objects, and language that surround it. A page about archival typesetting and a page about freight logistics should not be able to trade stylesheets. When you read a surface, the strongest signal of intent is that its palette, type, and layout trace to something specific about what it is. A surface whose choices would fit any neighbouring subject was designed against a vacuum; record that plainly.

---

## 2. THE HERO STATES A THESIS

A deliberate hero leads with whatever is most characteristic of the subject, in whatever form actually carries it — a sentence, an image, a moving thing, a working demo. The metric-plus-label-plus-gradient opening is the answer that arrives when no choice was made; it is the default to notice, not to celebrate. A typography-only hero can be fully intentional when the headline, copy, spacing, and primary action carry the thesis without decorative support. Capture the hero's actual signature move, not a normalized version of it.

---

## 3. STRUCTURE CARRIES MEANING

Eyebrows, dividers, numbers, and labels earn their place by encoding something true about the content. Numbered markers are the clearest tell: `01 / 02 / 03` belongs on material that genuinely is a sequence, where order tells the reader something. Applied to three unordered features it is decoration wearing the costume of information. When you record structure, note whether it means something or merely dresses the page.

---

## 4. DEVIATION IS EARNED ONE AT A TIME

Isolating every panel, accenting every call to action, giving every section its own treatment is not distinction, it is noise with ambition. One departure the subject can justify outperforms a surface where everything competes to be the signature. A surface with a single clear focal point read stronger than one with five.

---

## 5. MOTION IS A DECISION, NOT A FINISH

Purposeful motion has a job: an entrance that establishes hierarchy, a reveal that rewards scrolling, a hover that confirms a control is live. A single orchestrated moment usually reads as intent; scattered effects read as habit, and excess animation is one of the strongest signals that nobody was steering. Record the one move that carries the surface, and note when motion is merely everywhere.

---

## 6. COMPLEXITY MATCHES THE DIRECTION

Maximalism demands follow-through; minimalism demands precision, because with less on the surface every spacing and weight decision is exposed. Elegance is the chosen direction executed properly, not a particular density. Read a surface against its own ambition, not against a fixed amount of ornament.

---

## 7. RESTRAINT IS A SMALL BUDGET

Boldness is a budget, and it is small. Deliberate surfaces spend it on the signature and keep everything adjacent quiet enough that the signature reads. Decoration that cannot name the part of the subject it serves is the first thing to flag. Restraint is not the safe option either — a surface that risks nothing has made a choice, and usually a forgettable one.

---

## 8. WRITING IS DESIGN MATERIAL

Words are material, not trim. Copy named after what a person recognises and controls (someone manages notifications; nobody manages a webhook configuration), active voice, controls that state their consequence ("Save changes", not "Submit"), and one consistent name for one action across a flow are the marks of a surface whose words were designed. Generic copy makes a considered layout read as templated; capture the voice, not just the layout.
