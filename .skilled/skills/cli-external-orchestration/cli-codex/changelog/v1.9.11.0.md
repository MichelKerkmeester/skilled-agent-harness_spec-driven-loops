# v1.9.11.0, long prose lines become short ones

Readers met fifteen single lines. Each ran 400 to 1,300 characters. Rules 11 to 18 were walls of text. So were four dispatch gotchas and the memory handback paragraph. Each held several instructions inside one line. A reader had to separate them before acting. Every one is now short sentences and short lists. The commands, paths, flags, model ids and numbers are the ones that were already there.

&nbsp;

## What's New at a Glance

- The rules read in one pass.
  - Each numbered rule states its instruction first.
  - Its background comes after.
  - A rule with several steps carries them as a short list.
- The gotchas break at the idea.
  - Three gotchas each run as two or three lines.
  - They are the top-level `--search` flag, the `--sandbox workspace-write` socket limit and the `</dev/null` stdin rule.
  - The failure sits on one line.
  - The fix sits on the next.
- The manifest fields are a list.
  - The `DESIGN_DISPATCH_MANIFEST v1` block names its five fields one per line, not inside a sentence.
- Nothing was dropped.
  - Every command, flag, path, environment variable, model id and observed version appears exactly as before.
- Nothing routes differently.
  - No intent dictionary, resource map, loading level, guard or default changed.
  - The machine blocks are byte for byte what they were.

&nbsp;

## Upgrade

Nothing to do. The prose is the only change.
